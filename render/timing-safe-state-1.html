ECL Timing, Availability, and Safe-State Semantics 1

Version: 1.0 Status: normative companion of ECL-1 Version 1.0, a published version

Requirement identifiers are allocated in the order requirements were added and never reused, so a later identifier appears among earlier ones where its subject belongs: TS1-034 to TS1-036 follow TS1-009, and TS1-031 to TS1-033 follow TS1-018.

1. Time classes

ECL distinguishes:

  • issuer wall time;
  • observer wall time;
  • trusted external time;
  • monotonic duration;
  • causal order;
  • policy and revocation epoch.

TS1-001§ — A producer MUST identify the time class it records.

TS1-002§ — Wall time MUST use the restricted UTC RFC 3339 profile.

TS1-003§ — Monotonic evidence MUST identify the clock instance, ticks, and unit. It is comparable only within the declared clock domain.

TS1-004§ — A verifier MUST apply an explicit clock-skew and trusted-time policy.

TS1-005§ — Causal order and authenticated sequence evidence MUST take precedence over conflicting unauthenticated wall-clock order.

2. Latency declaration

TS1-006§ — An enforcement deployment profile MUST state the local-gate latency measurement points, percentile method, sampling interval, warm-up handling, hardware or runtime class, and action classes measured.

TS1-007§ — A product latency claim MUST identify the tested configuration and evidence. The standard does not define one universal performance number.

TS1-008§ — Remote synchronization, evidence export, and hosted policy management MUST NOT be included in the hot-path latency claim unless they are actually required before release.

TS1-009§ — A gate that exceeds its declared deadline MUST apply the timeout safe-state rule and record the breach.

TS1-034§ — Every declared timing bound MUST name its observation points (the events between which the interval is measured), its objective type (a maximum, or a stated percentile with its window), the clock that measures it, and the load and fault conditions under which it holds.

TS1-035§ — A revocation-propagation deadline and an emergency-halt acknowledgment bound are distinct obligations and MUST be declared separately; satisfying one MUST NOT be presented as satisfying the other.

TS1-036§ — A control acknowledgment bound (B) is the maximum interval, at the declared observation points, between a control operation's request and its durable acknowledgment, after which no effect depending on the changed scope or authority may commit; a deployment declaring B MUST state its termination or escalation mechanism for conflicting work.

3. Dependency failure

TS1-010§ — Each dependency MUST declare whether it is hot-path, cached, asynchronous, or advisory.

TS1-011§ — A local gate MUST be capable of applying the profile's decision and safe-state rules without an undeclared network dependency.

TS1-012§ — Policy, trust-root, registry, revocation, and evidence-export staleness budgets MUST be separately declared.

TS1-013§ — Exceeding a staleness budget MUST cause the specified restricted, degraded, deny, quarantine, or halt state.

TS1-014§ — Recovery after partition MUST reconcile policy epochs, revocations, consumed authorities, buffered evidence, unresolved effects, and clock discontinuities before normal mode resumes.

4. Fail behavior

TS1-015§ — Fail-open, fail-closed, restricted, degraded, quarantine, and emergency halt MUST be treated as distinct dispositions.

TS1-016§ — Fail-open MUST be an explicit action-class policy decision with owner, rationale, expiry, evidence obligations, and prohibited conditions.

TS1-017§ — Fail-open MUST NOT be used for an action when the invoked profile, law, contract, or irreversible-risk classification requires fail-closed.

TS1-018§ — Missing or invalid policy, identity, authority, integrity, critical extension, or pre-release evidence MUST fail closed.

TS1-031§ — A consumer MUST treat required proof as the set of inputs that TS1-018 names: policy, identity, authority, integrity, critical-extension handling, and pre-release evidence. A consumer MUST classify a dependency as advisory for an action exactly when its loss leaves required proof present, valid, and unexpired, and leaves locally validatable authority sufficient for that action.

TS1-032§ — A failure whose consequence is that any required proof is absent, invalid, expired, or unavailable MUST be classified as missing required proof and MUST fail closed under TS1-018, whatever its proximate cause. Reclassifying such a failure as a dependency outage, timeout, or other generic failure class in order to reach a fail-open disposition MUST NOT occur. The dispositions that fail closed are fail_closed, quarantine and emergency_halt: in a safe-state profile document, a dependency whose supplies_required_proof is true MUST declare one of them as its loss_disposition, and a consumer MUST reject any other disposition for it, restricted and degraded included, with ecl.rule.required-proof-dependency-fails-closed.v1.

TS1-033§ — A fail-open policy under TS1-016 MUST name the advisory dependencies it covers, and MUST NOT cover any dependency whose loss removes required proof.

The core standard sets which action classes a loss of required proof restricts and when the restriction ends (ECL1N-0191 to ECL1N-0193). Under the claim's dependency model, the loss of a dependency that only one action class requires restricts that class, and the loss of one that several classes require, directly or through other dependencies, restricts each of them. A loss whose scope cannot be established restricts the enclosing scope that the model declares, or the whole deployment where it declares none, and a critical integrity failure or an emergency control activation applies to the whole deployment. Within that scope each action is refused with a disposition that fails closed under TS1-032, and the dependency keeps its classification as one that supplies required proof (TS1-031).

TS1-019§ — Degraded mode MUST state what remains permitted, for how long, under which cached epochs, and how it expires into restricted mode or halt.

TS1-020§ — Restricted mode MUST state the bounded operations that remain available for diagnosis, recovery, or safe shutdown.

5. Buffering and backpressure

TS1-021§ — Local evidence buffering MUST declare capacity, durability, encryption, ordering, overflow, and export behavior.

TS1-022§ — Evidence buffer exhaustion MUST NOT silently drop verifier- critical events.

TS1-023§ — A deployment MUST declare whether exhaustion denies new actions, enters restricted mode, sheds only non-critical telemetry, or uses another profile-approved response.

TS1-024§ — Recovery MUST prove which buffered events were exported, rejected, duplicated, reordered, or remain unresolved.

6. Emergency and precedence

TS1-025§ — Emergency halt and quarantine MUST take precedence over ordinary admission and cached allow decisions.

TS1-026§ — Revocation, exhausted budget, legal hold, and critical verification failure precedence MUST be deterministic and profile-declared.

TS1-027§ — A lower-precedence decision MUST NOT revive an object placed in a higher-precedence safe state.

TS1-028§ — Emergency-channel availability and independence MUST be tested at the profile-defined interval and after material architecture change.

7. Evidence

TS1-029§ — Timing claims MUST retain raw observations, calculation method, exclusions, breaches, and environment identity.

TS1-030§ — Availability evidence MUST distinguish measured behavior, designed behavior, and untested assumption.
