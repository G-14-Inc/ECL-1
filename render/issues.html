ECL-1 normative issues

This index preserves the questions and their dispositions. Issues 001 and 002 were resolved earlier. Issues 003 through 007 are resolved in this candidate's requirements and assessment procedures as recorded below. A disposition is not publication authorization, an outside assessment or a legal opinion. Public comments and errata remain open under the governance process.

ECL-ISSUE-001: Class B, "and" versus "or"

Status: resolved: conjunctive.

The Class B definition in Section 4.5 required control approval and evidence verification in a separately administered trust domain; the Section 5.8 summary said or. The conjunctive reading was adopted: both control approval and evidence verification occur in a separately administered trust domain that the governed AI system and its ordinary operators cannot unilaterally modify. Class C builds on Class B: every Class B condition holds, and both functions are held by an externally independent trust domain or authority.

Sections 4.5 and 5.8 now state the same rule; ECL1N-0142 to ECL1N-0144 and checklist items IA-08 and IA-09 make it testable, and L2 and L3 claims, which require Class B, can be made.

ECL-ISSUE-002: Restricted mode for one action class's missing proof

Status: resolved in the text of Version 1.0 by ECL1N-0191 to ECL1N-0193.

The table of ECL1N-0146 (Section 8.8) gives "proof missing" as a trigger for restricted mode. The text did not say whether a missing proof that one action class requires places the whole deployment in restricted mode or only that class, while the safe-state profile schema declares dispositions for each action class, and docs/availability.md left the question to the assessor, so that two assessors could reach different scopes for the same fault.

The text now distinguishes an action-class restriction from a deployment-wide state (Section 4.2, Restricted Mode; Section 8.8). Under the claim's declared dependency model (ECL1N-0192), a loss of required proof that only one action class needs restricts that class, and the other classes continue only with their own required proof and controls intact; the failure of a dependency that several classes require restricts every class that requires it, directly or through other dependencies; a critical integrity failure or an emergency control activation brings the state that ECL1N-0146 gives it for the whole deployment, which no action-class scope or local exception overrides; and a loss whose scope cannot be established restricts the enclosing scope that the dependency model declares, or the whole deployment where it declares none (ECL1N-0191). A restriction ends only when required proof is obtained again after the fault and the authorization that the mode definitions declare for lifting it is recorded, and a deployment-wide state brought by an integrity failure or an emergency activation ends only by a recorded control action (ECL1N-0193). Required proof is never relabeled as advisory to keep a class outside the scope. Checklist item EA-29 tests the rule. The availability guide, TIMING-SAFE-STATE-1 and STRICT-FAILURE-1 state it in the same terms.

ECL-ISSUE-003: A verifiable input and an input the actor can influence

Status: resolved in the candidate requirements and assessment procedure.

Resolution: Section 4.3 and ECL1N-0178 distinguish actor-controlled assertions and bypassable validation from an independently validated state transition. An admitted action can affect a provider balance or sensor without making the authoritative observation actor-supplied. Source signatures do not launder assertions; freshness, selection and completeness remain explicit. EA-21 and the input cases in docs/decision-examples.md guide assessment.

Original question and requested scope (preserved for review):

The definition of a verifiable input (Section 4.3) and the one-way rule (ECL1N-0178 to ECL1N-0182) keep an actor from turning its own assertion into independent authority by storing, signing, summarizing or retrieving it. Read strictly, the same words could also exclude a value that the actor changes only through an admitted action, such as an independently kept bank balance, a budget that another service debits, or a sensor reading of a state that the action changed, although the actor cannot forge that record or bypass its rules.

A resolution is expected to define a verifiable input by who controls its authenticity and its state transitions under a declared protocol; to distinguish an actor that submits a request, causes a validated transition, chooses among genuine observations or asserts the value directly; to keep the prohibition on an actor-written field becoming trusted through a read; and to come with examples that cover actor-written fields, independently debited budgets, payment-provider balances, signed external mandates, sensors affected by the action, stale snapshots, selective omission and transformed memories.

ECL-ISSUE-004: What a person approving an action is shown

Status: resolved in the candidate requirements and assessment procedure.

Resolution: ECL1N-0174 and EA-17 require human-readable consequential fields, complete parameters accessible to the approver, canonical action binding, and a fresh approval after a consequential change. A digest supplements rather than replaces the presentation. Display ambiguity, hidden fields, localization and accessibility are covered in the assessment procedure.

Original question and requested scope (preserved for review):

ECL1N-0174 lets an approval presentation show the action's parameters or their digest. A digest alone cannot show an amount, a currency, a recipient, a deletion scope, an audience or a change of privilege that the parameters hold, and a minimum review time (ECL1N-0175) does not repair a presentation that leaves them out.

A resolution is expected to require a human-readable presentation of the consequential fields of the action and their consequences, exactly bound to the canonical action that is released, with the digest as a supplementary integrity reference; to state how units, currency, rounding, account identity, resource scope, truncation, localization and accessibility are presented and how display relates to execution; to require a new approval when a consequential field changes; and to come with tests of a harmless summary over a dangerous hidden parameter, look-alike account names, an amount changed after approval and an approved text whose destination changes.

ECL-ISSUE-005: Obligations at an OAuth resource server

Status: resolved in the candidate requirements and assessment procedure.

Resolution: OA1-008 explicitly limits Version 1.0 to admits with an empty obligations list. All three verifiers reject issuance carrying any obligation; the private issuer also refuses it. Three negative corpus cases cover before-release, during-execution and after-execution obligations. Fresh proof retrieval and critical token-condition modes are not defined by this version. Actions needing them use an appropriate DCMP-2 enforcement path.

Original question and requested scope (preserved for review):

OAUTH-AUTHORITY-1 lets a resource server enforce a verified admit without processing DCMP-2 (OA1-008, OA1-011). An admit can depend on a fresh state proof or another obligation that can only be met at release, and the profile does not say how each such obligation reaches the resource server.

A resolution is expected to name the supported ways: the issuer meets the obligation before it issues the token, where the obligation's validity window allows it; a critical token condition that the resource server enforces; or a fresh proof that the resource server obtains from a named authority at release; to require that a token is not issued when the selected way cannot carry the obligation; to state revocation, audience, action digest, proof freshness, clock tolerance and failure behavior at each hop; and to come with tests of a state change after issue, a policy change, a revoked grant, an expired proof, an unreachable proof service, an unsupported critical condition and a resource server that reads the token but cannot meet the obligation. A profile may support only some of these ways, provided that it states its capability boundary and that an obligation it does not support fails safely.

ECL-ISSUE-006: Evidence retention and lawful erasure

Status: resolved in the candidate requirements and assessment procedure.

Resolution: ECL1N-0050 gives a recorded legal-conflict procedure with qualified legal review, deadlines, minimization, lawful accountability records and assessment consequences. ECL1N-0051, FR-113 and the lifecycle hold/precedence rules use the same exception. Destroyed or unavailable evidence is not treated as a complete original bundle. This resolves the technical precedence contract; the published rights papers record the status of release rights review, and qualified review determines legal applicability.

Original question and requested scope (preserved for review):

The retention floor of ECL1N-0050 and FR-113, with the lifecycle rules of EVIDENCE-LIFECYCLE-1 (EL1-006, EL1-024, EL1-027), keeps evidence for at least the longest applicable period, while an applicable legal obligation can require content to be removed or minimized earlier. The text has no general procedure for such a conflict, and whether a given conflict exists depends on the record, its purpose and the applicable law.

A resolution is expected to rest on a legal review; to give a precedence rule and a procedure for transforming evidence when a conflict is real, which keeps the minimum lawful metadata and accountability evidence, records what was destroyed or restricted and states how a verification claim changes when its evidence is no longer available; to separate source content, personal identifiers, operational metadata, cryptographic commitments and destruction records, and not to call a digest anonymous by default; and to state roles, authorization, contested requests, legal holds, the consequences for an assessment, and how a verifier reports evidence that is unavailable on purpose without claiming that the bundle is complete.

ECL-ISSUE-007: The shared-root prohibition and the independence classes

Status: resolved in the candidate requirements and assessment procedure.

Resolution: FR-001's single-power prohibition remains intact at every class. ECL1N-0056 requires a capability matrix including administrators and host/hypervisor roots. Class A permits one operator, without permitting one power to alter both planes. Co-location satisfies no class if its shared root can alter both. Class B adds separate administration of both approval and verification; Class C adds external independence. IA-01 tests the matrix.

Original question and requested scope (preserved for review):

FR-001 forbids a single credential, role, privilege domain or trust root that is sufficient to alter both the governed AI system's behavior and the control outcomes, while the independence classes of Section 4.5 and the packaging constraints of Section 5.8 allow some shared hosting at Class A. Read side by side, the general prohibition can seem stronger than the class allowances, and the texts do not use the same terms for an actor's privileges, an administrator's privileges, custody of a root of trust and a compromise of the host.

A resolution is expected to publish, for each independence class, what the actor can do, what an operator can change, what needs another administrator, which credentials are separate and what a compromise of the host defeats, and to make the general prohibition and the class allowances refer to the same capabilities, keeping the existing disclosure and assessment obligations.
