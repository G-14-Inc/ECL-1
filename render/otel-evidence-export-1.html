ECL OpenTelemetry Evidence Export Profile 1

Requirement-IDs: compiled Version: 1.0 Status: normative profile of ECL-1 Version 1.0, a published version

This profile copies ECL decisions and evidence into OpenTelemetry log records so that observability tools and security information and event management (SIEM) systems can ingest them. The export is a copy. The signed evidence bundle remains the record that a claim relies on, and a third party still verifies the bundle, not the copy. The exporter sits after the evidence path: it reads objects once they are recorded, and its failure changes no decision and blocks no evidence. Each record carries the identifiers of the request, response and action it belongs to and, when the deployment holds an export key, a digest of the object keyed with that key, so that a consumer can match the record to the bundle without the telemetry letting its readers test guesses about what the object holds.

The profile adds requirements to a claim that declares it. It removes none of the core requirements or of the requirements of DCMP-2 and EBMP-2.

This profile has no release boundary in the sense of ECL1N-0185: the exporter copies evidence after it is recorded and releases no action (OE1-002).

The ecl. attribute namespace is not a namespace of the pinned semantic conventions. The naming guidance there recommends a prefix that the defining organization controls; the profile keeps ecl., the prefix of ECL-1's names. A change of prefix would go through the change process of ECL-1.

Copy, not record

OE1-001§ — Exported telemetry MUST be a copy of ECL evidence: the signed evidence bundle remains the record for every claim, and a log record, span event or other exported item MUST NOT be offered as the evidence that ECL1N-0047, FR-107 or FR-108 requires.

OE1-002§ — Apart from the response of a detection declared under ECL1N-0167, an export failure, such as an unreachable collector, a full buffer, a serialization error or a crash of the exporter, MUST NOT change a decision, delay or block the release of an admitted action, or change, delay or block the recording of evidence: the exporter MUST read an object only after it is recorded and MUST NOT be a dependency of the decision or enforcement path. A deployment that reacts to the loss of its monitoring path MUST do so through such a declared detection, whose response is recorded as its own control action.

Log records

OE1-003§ — Each exported DCMP-2 message or EBMP-2 event MUST become one OpenTelemetry log record whose EventName is ecl.dcmp. followed by the message type or ecl.ebmp. followed by the event type, whose Timestamp is the object's issued_at or observed_at, whose ObservedTimestamp is the time at which the exporter read the object and is not earlier than Timestamp, and whose attributes, in which no key appears more than once, are those of the attribute table below for that object, each equal to the value in the object it copies and held in the OTLP AnyValue kind that the table gives for it.

OE1-004§ — Each log record MUST carry the identifiers of its workflow, request, response and action where the object has them, and MUST NOT carry a digest that can be computed without the export key of OE1-010, such as the SHA-256 digest of the object it copies or the parameters digest that the object carries, other than the policy digest of the attribute table, which identifies the policy that decided as the policy's identifier and version do; a consumer that holds the export key MUST treat a record whose keyed digest differs from the one it computes over the bundle's object as not describing that object.

OE1-005§ — A log record MUST NOT carry action parameters, tool arguments or results, constraints, credentials, tokens, signatures, key material, the free text of reasons, the identifiers of people and records (ecl.actor.id, ecl.subject.id and ecl.action.target), or other non-public data, in its attributes or its Body, unless the export policy declares the attribute and the data class it carries (EBMP2-034, EBMP2-035); an exporter MUST NOT emit a log record attribute that is neither in the attribute table nor declared, an attribute that the table exports only when declared without that declaration, or a Body that the export policy does not declare.

OE1-006§ — When the decision and enforcement path is traced, an exporter MAY also add the record's attributes to a span event named as its EventName on the span of the governed operation, and MAY set the record's TraceId and SpanId to that span; a span event MUST NOT replace the log record.

OE1-007§ — An exporter that writes to a file or to standard output MUST write one OTLP LogsData object per line in JSON Lines form, encoded under the pinned OTLP JSON Protobuf encoding: trace and span identifiers in hexadecimal, enum values as integers, and 64-bit integers, including timestamps, as decimal strings.

OE1-008§ — The SeverityNumber of a record MUST follow the severity table below, and its SeverityText MUST be the short name that the table gives for that number (INFO, WARN or ERROR); a consumer MUST NOT infer authorization, release or the absence of an effect from a record's severity or from the presence or absence of a record.

OE1-009§ — An exporter that retains export records MUST write each one as ecl_otel_export_record_v1, valid against https://externalcontrollayer.ai/ns/schema/binding/otel-export/1.

Keyed digests

OE1-010§ — An exporter MUST NOT emit ecl.object.digest, ecl.action.parameters_digest or ecl.export.key_id unless the deployment holds an export key: a secret of at least 32 bytes from a cryptographically secure random source, generated for that deployment's telemetry export alone, held only by the exporter and by the consumers that match records to the bundle, never readable by the governed AI system, and never a key that signs or verifies ECL evidence. With an export key, every record MUST carry the key's identifier, a string, as the stringValue of ecl.export.key_id, which MUST NOT be derived from the key; in ecl.object.digest, the HMAC-SHA-256 (RFC 2104) under the key of the restricted canonical JSON of the object (IE1-001); and, where the object has a parameters digest, in ecl.action.parameters_digest, the HMAC-SHA-256 under the key of the restricted canonical JSON of that digest; each HMAC as the stringValue of its 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits. A consumer that does not hold the key that a record names MUST still treat as not describing the object a record whose keyed digests are not in that form, or that lacks a keyed digest that the object has.

An unkeyed digest of an object, or the parameters digest that a request or an execution event carries, lets a reader of the telemetry who can list the possible values, such as an amount or a short list of accounts, compute the digest of each candidate and confirm which one was used. A digest keyed with a secret that the telemetry's readers do not hold still lets a consumer that holds the key match a record to the bundle, and it gives everyone else nothing to compare a guess with. The digests inside the signed evidence bundle are not keyed and do not change. Replacing the export key changes its identifier, so a consumer knows which key a record needs. An exporter without an export key exports no digest, and a consumer then matches records to the bundle by their identifiers.

Attribute table

Every attribute value is a string held as an OTLP stringValue, except ecl.workflow.attempt and ecl.workflow.sequence, which are integers held as an intValue (OE1-003, OE1-007); an attribute held in another AnyValue kind, such as a bytesValue that holds the same text, does not hold the value that the table gives. String values copy the object's value. Each attribute key appears once in a record (OE1-003), as the pinned logs.proto requires of a log record's attributes. A keyed digest is a stringValue of 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits (OE1-010), a form that a consumer without the export key can check, though it cannot compare the digest with the object. Attributes whose source member is absent are left out, and a row applies only to the objects it names, also when another object holds the same member. Execution events are the EBMP-2 events of kind execution (release_prepared, execution_released, execution_observed and execution_terminal), decision events those of kind decision (decision_recorded), authority events those of kind authority (authority_issued and authority_consumed), request events those of kind request (request_received), and terminal events those of type execution_terminal. The rows marked "when declared" carry the identifiers of people and records: an exporter emits each of them only when the export policy declares it with the data class it carries (OE1-005), and then for every object that the row names and that holds the source member.

Mapping to OCSF (informative)

A consumer that normalizes to the Open Cybersecurity Schema Framework (OCSF, schema 1.9.0, tag 1.9.0, commit 856d462bd20dc46cc1ffed2dfffe3b91ef0fbeba) can map a decision response record to the API Activity class (class_uid 6003): time from Timestamp, action_id 1 (Allowed) for admit and 2 (Denied) for deny, action_id 99 (Other) with the outcome in action for hold and challenge, disposition_id 1 (Allowed), 2 (Blocked) or 14 (Delayed), and the ecl. attributes in unmapped. An execution_terminal record maps to the same class with its state in status_detail. OCSF records made this way are copies of copies: for a consumer that holds the export key, the keyed digest in ecl.object.digest is what connects them to the signed bundle, and otherwise only their identifiers do.

Export record (informative)

An ecl_otel_export_record_v1 record holds one export: the object the exporter read; the OTLP log_record it made; the bundle_id of the bundle that holds the object, which a log record that carries ecl.evidence.bundle copies; the declared_attributes of the export policy, each with the data class it carries, where the name body declares a log record body; the export outcome (exported or failed) and when it was attempted; and evidence_after, the same object as the evidence held it after the attempt. The rules registered for this profile, whose names begin ecl.rule.otel-, check a record after its schema; a checker compares a keyed digest only under an export key that its caller supplies, and recognizes a digest that needs no key without one.

Security considerations

Telemetry pipelines are wider and less protected than the evidence store, and their contents reach people and systems outside the claim's trust boundary. The profile therefore exports identifiers, outcomes and, with an export key, keyed digests, and leaves parameters, results, reason text and every digest that needs no key in the bundle, apart from the policy digest, which identifies the policy as its identifier and version do. A digest of low-entropy content lets a reader confirm guessed values; this profile exports such digests only under an export key (OE1-010), and exports the identifiers of people and records only when the export policy declares them. A record can be forged, dropped or delayed anywhere along the pipeline, so its only authority is the keyed digest that a consumer that holds the export key compares with the signed bundle. A reader who obtains the export key can test guesses as an unkeyed digest would let them, which is why the key stays with the exporter and those consumers. The object_id of an EBMP-2 event is derived from the event's content (EBMP2-047) and is exported as its identifier; confirming a guess through it needs every member of the event, including the digests of the objects it references, which no record carries. Making release wait for an export would let anyone who can disrupt the pipeline stop or slow governed actions, and a deny that depended on export would turn monitoring into a control path that the claim does not declare; both are excluded by OE1-002.
