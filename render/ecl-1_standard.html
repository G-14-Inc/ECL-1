The ECL-1 Guarantee (Informative)

The guarantee. A deployment that conforms to ECL-1 under a full-system or bounded action-class mediation claim provides the following for each consequential action that the claim declares in scope. The action crosses an enforcement boundary that the governed AI system cannot bypass, under a decision that the governed AI system cannot change. A failure that leaves the action without required proof, including an integrity failure, policy ambiguity or a decision service that does not answer, ends in denial, restricted mode or halt, and never in the action going ahead. Each decision leaves tamper-evident evidence that a third party can check and, from L2, verify offline outside the primary control plane. The guarantee holds only within the boundary, level, independence class and trust assumptions that the claim states, and only as far as the claim's assessment establishes it (Section 7.8): at L1 the absence of bypass rests on the documented enumeration method and exercised interception points (ECL1N-0039), and from L2 also on the assessor's negative tests (FR-101, ECL1N-0151). A component or profile implementation claim does not provide the guarantee.

This section adds no requirement; the normative rules are in Sections 2 to 11. Terms in this section are defined in Section 4. The guarantee has seven elements, each listed below with the requirements that carry it, and registries/guarantee-trace.json records the elements that each compiled requirement serves. The section ends with who the standard is for, what it leaves to other tools, and how to read the rest.

  1. Mediation without bypass. Every in-scope action crosses the Enforcement Boundary before its effect, no undisclosed path goes around it, and the governed AI system can neither rewrite, disable, nor bypass enforcement (ECL1N-0028, ECL1N-0033, ECL1N-0046, ECL1N-0141). A claim shows this only as far as its assessment establishes it: at L1 through the documented path-enumeration method and exercised interception points (ECL1N-0039), which checklist item EA-25 tests, and from L2 also through the assessor's negative tests of the enforcement path (FR-101, ECL1N-0151).
  2. A decision the governed AI system cannot change. Policy outside the governed AI system decides. Inputs whose asserted values the system controls, and judgments about meaning or risk, can make an outcome stricter but are never the basis of an admit, and the control plane is independent of the governed AI system (FR-001, ECL1N-0040, ECL1N-0178 to ECL1N-0182, Section 7.5).
  3. Failure ends closed. Missing proof, an integrity failure, policy ambiguity, and the loss of a required dependency end in denial, restricted mode, or halt (FR-003, ECL1N-0043, Section 8.8). A loss of required proof restricts the action classes that need that proof, and a critical integrity failure or an emergency activation stops the whole deployment (ECL1N-0191).
  4. Bounded, revocable authority. Authority is typed, transaction-bound, time-bounded, and revocable, and each delegation hop preserves or narrows it (ECL1N-0031, ECL1N-0035, ECL1N-0042). An enforcement point releases an action only after establishing, at the action's release boundary, that its authority is usable there and that every obligation and other check the release depends on is met (ECL1N-0186 to ECL1N-0188).
  5. Evidence a third party can check. Each decision and action leaves tamper-evident evidence, including the inputs the decision rested on (ECL1N-0037, ECL1N-0047, ECL1N-0181). From L2, that evidence is independently verifiable outside the primary control plane (FR-108), and the verifier checks a signed bundle offline under its own trust policy (EBMP-2 section 11). An enforcement point keeps what it needs to recover an attempt before it releases the action (ECL1N-0189), and a verifier reports a check that it did not make as unchecked (ECL1N-0194).
  6. Declared scope and claim boundary. A claim states which actions and paths are in scope, which are excluded, its level, its independence class, and how it was assessed, so that no reader takes it to cover more (Section 2.5, Section 7).
  7. Operations that keep it true. Revocation propagates within a declared timing case, an emergency control plane halts without the governed AI system's cooperation, declared detections hold or stop the affected actions, and changes to controls are reviewed by another person (FR-004, Section 8.7, ECL1N-0053, ECL1N-0166 to ECL1N-0170).

Who the standard is for. Organizations that deploy AI systems able to take consequential actions, the engineers who build the control layer around them, the assessors who check a claim, and the buyers and regulators who rely on one. Section 2 decides whether a system is in scope.

What it leaves to other tools. ECL-1 does not judge what an action means, evaluate model quality, analyze conversations, operate security monitoring, provide containment substrates, or govern the organization. Those functions connect to ECL-1 through defined interfaces: a judgment about meaning or risk enters a decision as a judgment input that can only make the outcome stricter (Section 5.2), evidence is exported to monitoring tools, and sandboxes, guardian agents, gateways, and policy engines act as decision sources or enforcement points under ECL-1's rules. Section 2.10 lists each function and how it connects.

How to read the rest. Section 2 sets scope and the claim boundary. Section 5 states the runtime control invariant and the six control responsibilities; Section 6 holds the functional requirements by level; Section 7 the conformance levels, independence classes, and assessment; Section 8 deployment models, timing, and fallback states; Section 9 the threat model and evidence-signing key custody; Section 10 the relationship to other frameworks; and Section 11 the checklist that an assessor scores. Section 1.1 suggests a reading order by role, and docs/l1-minimum.md lists every requirement and checklist item that an L1 claim answers.

1. Executive Summary

ECL-1 requires that each consequential autonomous action be authorized, enforced and recorded at the boundary where it takes effect.

ECL-1 is a runtime control standard for autonomous or semi-autonomous systems that can produce consequential external effects. It standardizes the control architecture, claim boundary, evidence model, and assessment rules needed to govern consequential AI action at the moment of execution.

ECL-1 standardizes six control responsibilities:

  1. Decision
  2. Authority
  3. Enforcement
  4. Provenance
  5. Verification
  6. Fleet

The first four are runtime action-governance responsibilities. Verification and Fleet are supervisory control planes. ECL-1 therefore standardizes both the inline execution choke point and the supervisory mechanisms that keep that choke point trustworthy over time.

This document is not a general AI governance framework. It does not replace management systems, legal interpretation, model-quality evaluation, or sector-specific safety certification. It defines a runtime-control layer that operates alongside those frameworks and regimes.

ECL-1 distinguishes:

  • full-system mediation claims from bounded action-class claims;
  • soft enforcement from hard enforcement;
  • ordinary runtime mediation from the emergency control plane;
  • restricted mode from degraded mode;
  • prose architecture from machine-testable interoperability artifacts.

The architectural thesis and policy rationale for this standard are presented in a companion paper, listed in the Bibliography as [TILLEY]:

  • Tilley, R. F., Jr. The External Control Problem. Unpublished manuscript, G-14, Inc., March 2026.

1.1 How to Read This Standard

Procurement officers and program managers should start with Section 11, the Conformance Checklist, then read Section 7 to understand conformance levels, claim classes, and assessment status boundaries.

Architects and engineers should start with Section 5, Architectural Components, then read Section 6 for the normative runtime requirements and Section 8 for deployment-specific timing and operating constraints.

1.2 Scope and Limitations of Version 1.0

Version 1.0 standardizes the runtime control boundary for consequential AI actions, including pre-action mediation, typed authority, tamper-evident provenance, verification-triggered response behavior, and bounded claim logic.

Version 1.0 does not by itself constitute legal certification, sector-safety certification, model-quality assurance, or organization-wide AI governance. It is meant to be used alongside broader management, legal, safety, and sector-specific controls where those controls apply.

Version 1.0 also assumes that concrete conformance claims will be made through separate implementation, evidence, and assessment packages. Those packages may vary in maturity and independence; the core standard text is not itself a claim that any specific deployment has satisfied the required assessment posture.

At every level, Version 1.0 is not complete without the version-locked DCMP-2, EBMP-2, ECL Integrity Envelope 1, extension registries, semantic rules, and conformance vectors published with the same release. ECL1N-0057, ECL1N-0117, ECL1N-0118, and ECL1N-0181 apply those companions to every claim, so an L1 claim answers each of their requirements that applies at L1 (ECL1N-0022, Section 6.1); docs/l1-minimum.md lists them. The core Markdown export alone is therefore not sufficient for assessment at any level unless the companion package is published concurrently, cited by version, and bound to the core source through the release manifest.

2. Scope and Applicability

This section defines where ECL-1 applies and how conformance scope is bounded. Section 4 states how the normative keywords are interpreted.

2.1 Purpose

ECL-1 applies to runtime control architecture for consequential AI action, centered on the six-responsibility model: Decision, Authority, Enforcement, Provenance, Verification, and Fleet.

The purpose of ECL-1 is to close the governance-to-runtime gap by defining the control boundary, evidence rules, and assessment structure needed to govern consequential actions at action time.

2.2 Systems In Scope

ECL1N-0001§ — A system MUST be treated as in scope for ECL-1 when all conditions below are true:

  1. The AI system can initiate or materially advance actions without per-action human authorization.
  2. One or more actions are consequential under Section 2.4.
  3. The operating environment is open-world or partially open-world, meaning tools, inputs, plugins, network peers, or contextual state are not fully closed and enumerable at design time.

ECL1N-0002§ — If scope classification is uncertain, the deployment MUST be classified as in scope until a documented review concludes otherwise.

2.3 Systems Out of Scope

The following are out of scope for direct ECL-1 conformance claims:

  • AI systems used only for content generation with no execution authority and no non-public data egress path beyond ordinary user viewing.
  • Systems where a human authorizes every consequential action and the AI does not execute independently or queue execution for later release.
  • Isolated research sandboxes with no path to production data, production credentials, production networks, or production actuators.
  • Closed-world finite-state systems whose consequential behavior is already fully governed by a sector-specific safety standard and where ECL mediation is not part of the architecture.

ECL1N-0003§ — Out-of-scope classification MUST NOT be used as a substitute for legal, regulatory, or contractual obligations.

2.4 Consequential Action Classification

ECL1N-0004§ — An action MUST be classified as consequential if any of the following is true:

  1. It mutates external state.
  2. It discloses, routes, exports, or transforms non-public data outside the agent's isolated execution context.
  3. It creates, commits, or authorizes financial, legal, contractual, identity, infrastructure, network, or physical effects.
  4. It expands authority, execution reach, or available tool/resource access.
  5. It invokes an external service, system, or actuator in a way that creates material operator cost, obligation, or exposure.

ECL1N-0005§ — An action MAY be classified as non-consequential only when all of the following are true:

  1. It remains fully confined to an isolated execution context.
  2. It does not disclose non-public data outside that context.
  3. It does not mutate external state or commit external resources.
  4. It does not expand authority or execution reach.

ECL1N-0006§ — A local draft rewrite that remains fully confined to an isolated execution context, discloses no non-public data, mutates no external state, and expands no authority MAY be classified as non-consequential.

Informative examples (non-normative):

  • A read-only query that exports non-public customer, security, or operational data outside the isolated execution context is still consequential because it creates disclosure and operator exposure even when it does not mutate external state.

ECL1N-0007§ — Each claim MUST publish a Consequential Action Register containing:

  • action class identifier;
  • classification decision (consequential or non-consequential);
  • rationale tied to the rule set above;
  • affected resources, identities, and data classes;
  • required enforcement class (soft or hard);
  • applicable human-approval threshold, if any.

ECL1N-0008§ — For full-system-mediation and bounded-action-class-mediation claims, the Consequential Action Register MUST be validated against the actual runtime capability inventory, including tool manifests, API permissions, delegated authority paths, workflow triggers, and external service bindings reachable by the SoI. Any reachable capability path not represented in the register MUST be included in scope or disclosed in the Negative Boundary Annex.

2.5 Conformance Claim Boundary and Claim Classes

ECL1N-0009§ — A conformance claim MUST define a System of Interest (SoI) boundary that includes:

  • the claim class;
  • the Consequential Action Register;
  • all interfaces and execution paths in scope;
  • the deployment topology in scope;
  • the control responsibilities in scope;
  • the trust boundaries and independence class in scope.

The allowed claim classes are:

  • Full-System Mediation Claim: all reachable consequential action paths for the SoI are in scope and mediated.
  • Bounded Action-Class Mediation Claim: only named consequential action classes are in scope and mediated.
  • Component or Profile Implementation Claim: one control component or one interoperability profile is implemented without asserting full mediation of a runtime SoI.

Only a mediation claim, full-system or bounded, provides the ECL-1 guarantee, within the boundary it declares and as far as its assessment establishes it.

ECL1N-0010§ — Every claim MUST include a Negative Boundary Annex listing:

  • excluded effect channels;
  • excluded tools, agents, or resources;
  • shared runtime or admin surfaces not covered by the claim;
  • any known alternate paths from digital intent to external effect.

ECL1N-0011§ — If an external-effect path is not explicitly included in the SoI or disclosed in the Negative Boundary Annex, the claim is incomplete and MUST be treated as invalid.

2.7 Scope Constraints and Non-Goals

ECL-1 is a runtime safety and control architecture standard. It is not a complete substitute for:

  • model quality evaluation and bias assessment;
  • organizational governance management systems;
  • legal compliance interpretation;
  • sector-specific functional safety certification.

Section 2.10 lists the functions that ECL-1 leaves to other tools by design and how each one connects to an ECL-1 deployment.

ECL1N-0012§ — Conformance to ECL-1 MUST NOT be represented as proof that all liveness, performance, or business-continuity objectives are guaranteed.

2.8 Boundary Clarifications

  • Vertical safety or industrial standards remain co-normative when the system still performs open-world or partially open-world consequential actions through software-controlled tools, APIs, networks, or actuators.
  • ECL1N-0013§ — A Consequential Action Trace MUST use one root trace identifier for the approval-to-effect chain.
  • ECL1N-0014§ — Each external effect MUST have a unique action identifier. Child actions MUST preserve parent linkage rather than collapsing all fan-out into one flat action record.
  • Single-instance deployments still implement Fleet responsibilities as a fleet-of-one. Absence of horizontal scale does not remove revocation, versioning, or coordinated-response obligations.
  • ECL1N-0015§ — Model-routing, multi-model, or model-switching architectures MUST treat each model that can originate, transform, or approve consequential actions for the SoI as part of the declared SoI. Consequential-action classification, boundary disclosure, and enforcement obligations apply regardless of which model instance produced the action proposal.

2.9 Conversational and Language-Mediated Systems

ECL1N-0016§ — Conversational systems are not automatically out of scope. A deployment MAY be treated as out of scope for direct ECL-1 conformance only when all of the following are true:

  1. output is view-only or draft-only;
  2. output does not disclose non-public data outside permitted viewing;
  3. output does not itself create legal, financial, contractual, identity, or operational effect;
  4. output is not consumed as an action channel by another system, workflow, or operator with queued or automatic effect; and
  5. the deployment does not invoke or materially advance consequential actions through tools, APIs, workflow triggers, or delegated execution paths.

ECL1N-0017§ — A conversational or language-mediated system MUST be treated as in scope when its output can:

  1. disclose non-public data;
  2. trigger, queue, parameterize, or materially advance a consequential tool/API/workflow action;
  3. serve as an approval, denial, instruction, notice, or commitment that creates consequential effect; or
  4. materially expand authority, downstream execution reach, or available external resource access.

ECL1N-0018§ — Prompt injection, jailbreaks, hostile retrieved content, and hostile tool responses are language-origin threats, but ECL-1 does not attempt to standardize against every possible hostile string pattern. Instead, conversational inputs and machine-consumable outputs MUST be treated as untrusted whenever they can influence consequential action release, protected-data disclosure, or downstream execution.

ECL1N-0019§ — A public FAQ bot with no tools, no protected-data access, and no downstream workflow or execution path MAY be out of scope.

ECL1N-0020§ — A draft-only assistant whose human separately reviews and authorizes every consequential send or execution step MAY remain out of scope for direct ECL-1 conformance, but the downstream execution system may still be in scope.

ECL1N-0163§ — When output that a conversational system sends in the organization's name can state a price, term, policy, eligibility, legal, or health matter on which a recipient may rely, sending that output MUST be treated as a consequential action under ECL1N-0017, even where ECL1N-0019 would otherwise permit the system to be out of scope, and the decision to release it MUST either compare the statement with approved reference content or obtain an authorized person's approval.

Informative examples (non-normative):

  • A support chatbot that can surface customer account, case, or operational data is in scope for those disclosure paths even if it never calls an actuator.
  • A conversational agent that can send email, create tickets, issue denials, trigger transactions, or pass structured instructions into another system is in scope for those effect paths even when its primary interface is natural language.

2.10 Outside ECL-1 by Design (Informative)

ECL-1 standardizes one layer: the decision, authority, enforcement, and evidence around each consequential action. It leaves the functions below to other tools and states how each one connects. No connection changes the guarantee: whatever these tools report, an admit rests on policy evaluated over verifiable inputs (ECL1N-0180), and a failure that removes required proof ends closed.

Function left to other tools Examples How it connects to ECL-1
Meaning and intent analysis Intent alignment, accumulated session context, interpretation of natural-language instructions As a declared judgment input (ECL1N-0178 to ECL1N-0182), which can make an outcome stricter and is never the basis of an admit, or as a declared detection whose response applies under ECL1N-0167 and ECL1N-0168.
Semantic drift and anomaly detection Embedding drift, behavioral baselines, risk scores As a declared judgment input or a declared detection, as for meaning and intent analysis.
Conversation and session analytics Transcript review, topic and sentiment analysis As a declared judgment input or a declared detection; a finding about a past session reaches later decisions only as a declared detection.
Model quality evaluation Accuracy, bias, and robustness evaluation of a model Outside runtime control (Section 2.7). Its results can inform policy and the Consequential Action Register. For L2 and above, a model change is recorded under ECL1N-0171.
Observability and security monitoring Telemetry pipelines, dashboards, security information and event management (SIEM) As consumers of exported evidence, through the profile OTEL-EVIDENCE-EXPORT-1. An alert that indicates wrong behavior returns as a declared detection (ECL1N-0167).
Sandboxes and other containment substrates Process, file-system, and network isolation of an agent As enforcement points for the actions they mediate, when they meet the Enforcement Layer requirements (ECL1N-0043) and fail closed.

4. Terms and Definitions

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

The following definitions are normative for this standard.

4.1 Architecture Terms

ECL-1: This standard for the External Control Layer.

External Control Layer (ECL): An instantiated runtime control infrastructure that implements the architecture defined by ECL-1 for governing consequential actions.

Semi-Autonomous System: A system that can originate, queue, sequence, materially shape, or materially advance consequential actions without per-action human authorization even when a human remains responsible for broader objectives or can intervene.

Governed AI System: The AI system whose consequential actions a claim governs: its models, the runtime that executes their outputs, and every tool, plugin, memory store, credential, and process that the runtime can invoke, write, or reconfigure, other than the components of the External Control Layer. The terms "governed runtime", "governed AI runtime", and "governed actor" mean the governed AI system.

ECL Architecture: The vendor-neutral six-responsibility architecture specified by ECL-1, comprising Decision, Authority, Enforcement, Provenance, Verification, and Fleet responsibilities.

Decision Layer: The control responsibility that evaluates proposed consequential actions against active policy and emits admit, deny, hold, or challenge outcomes.

Authority Layer: The control responsibility that manages principal identity context, typed authority, delegation constraints, and credential lifecycle required for action approval.

Enforcement Layer: The control responsibility at the execution boundary where action requests are intercepted and control outcomes are applied.

Provenance Layer: The control responsibility that records immutable or tamper-evident action evidence and lineage metadata.

Verification Layer: The supervisory control plane that checks whether ECL controls are operating correctly and have not drifted from approved posture.

Fleet Layer: The supervisory control plane that governs policy, revocation, and coordinated response across one or more instances of the governed AI system.

Consequence-Coupling Controls: Cross-layer controls that constrain the coupling between model cognition and real-world effect, including independent state witness, capability freeze, consequence budgets, irreversibility-aware commit modes, and heterogeneous checker quorum.

State Witness: An externally sourced or independently verified fact about preconditions, postconditions, or relevant world state used to determine whether a consequential action may proceed or whether its declared outcome actually occurred.

State Witness Register: The profile or package artifact that names the precondition and postcondition witnesses required for a bounded action class, their sources, freshness limits, disagreement handling, and trust assumptions.

4.2 Control and Security Terms

Consequential Action: An action that meets one or more of the inclusion tests in Section 2.4. Mutation of external state is sufficient but not necessary; sensitive-data disclosure, authority expansion, cost/obligation creation, and external-service invocation can also make an action consequential.

Materially Advance: To prepare, sequence, authorize, queue, parameterize, or trigger a step in a way that meaningfully increases the likelihood or readiness of a consequential external effect, even if the system does not perform the final effecting operation itself.

Consequential Action Register: The published inventory of action classes, classification decisions, resource/data impact, enforcement class, and rationale used to determine claim scope.

Enforcement Boundary: The architectural boundary that in-scope consequential actions cross before execution (ECL1N-0028).

Reference Monitor Property Set: Always invoked, tamper-resistant, and bounded enough for analysis and test.

Complete Mediation: The property that no in-scope consequential action bypasses the enforcement boundary.

Fail-Closed: The property that missing proof, integrity failure, policy ambiguity, or enforcement uncertainty results in deny, restricted mode, or halt rather than allow.

Restricted Mode: The enforced operating state in which new consequential actions are denied except for an explicit safe-state or containment allowlist. It applies to a scope of action classes: every in-scope action class, unless a requirement of this standard sets a narrower scope, as ECL1N-0191 does for a loss of required proof. Actions of classes outside the scope continue only under their own controls.

Degraded Mode: The operating state in which ordinary control dependencies are impaired but bounded local control state remains valid for a limited window (ECL1N-0029).

Emergency Control Plane: The narrowly scoped control path used for halt, override, or quarantine actions when ordinary runtime mediation cannot safely continue. It is separately authenticated, separately audited, and not a general-purpose bypass path.

Ordinary Runtime Mediation: The normal Decision, Authority, Enforcement, and Provenance path used for consequential actions during non-emergency operation. It excludes the emergency control plane and any exceptional containment-only authority.

Safety Property: A policy property where violation appears as a finite bad prefix of execution.

Liveness Property: A property asserting eventual progress; not generally enforceable by the same runtime mechanisms used for safety-property controls.

4.3 Decision and Authority Terms

Policy Decision Point (PDP): Decision sub-component that computes policy outcomes.

Policy Enforcement Point (PEP): Enforcement sub-component that applies policy outcomes at the boundary.

Policy Administration Function: Governance function that versions, approves, and distributes policy.

Decision Contract: A versioned machine-readable schema for decision-request/decision-response exchange.

Decision Input: A fact, record, instruction, signal, or score that the Decision Layer evaluates to reach the outcome for a proposed consequential action. The proposed action's operation, target, and the parameter values that define its effect are not decision inputs: they are the subject of the decision, which binds them by digest (DCMP2-007, DCMP2-044). Any statement in a request about authority, approval, intent, risk, or the state of the world is a decision input.

Decision Input Class: The class of a decision input that the Consequential Action Register declares (ECL1N-0178): verifiable, actor_supplied, or judgment. verifiable is the most trusted class and actor_supplied the least, with judgment between them. The output of a probabilistic model or heuristic detector about meaning or risk is a judgment input, whatever it was computed from. Any other input computed from other decision inputs takes the least trusted class among them; the request's operation, target, and parameters do not lower it. An input computed from an input that is unavailable, late, or malformed counts as not available itself (DCMP2-053), and no input is computed from itself, directly or through other inputs.

Verifiable Input: A decision input of the class verifiable: a value whose authenticity and permitted state transitions are controlled by an authority that the governed AI system cannot modify or bypass. The Decision Layer obtains it from that authority, or authenticates its origin under a key the governed AI system does not hold. An admitted action can cause a transition that the authority independently validates, such as a debit of a budget or a change to a payment-provider balance; that causal influence alone does not make the value actor-supplied. A source signature, storage, retrieval or transformation does not give independent authority to a field whose asserted value the actor controls. The authority's validation rules, freshness and completeness determine what the value establishes; authenticity alone does not establish truth or completeness. Examples are a system of record, an instruction or mandate that a principal authenticates to the control plane with a credential the governed AI system does not hold, a consequence budget ledger, a signed state witness, and the policy and trust state. The evidence of the decision identifies its value by reference and digest (ECL1N-0181).

Actor-Supplied Input: A decision input of the class actor_supplied: one whose asserted value the governed AI system produces or can overwrite without an independent authority validating the assertion or the state transition, or whose authenticity or transition checks it can modify or bypass. Examples include its own statements about an action's purpose, authority, approval or the state of the world, including a statement returned from storage or signed by a service that attests only to receiving it.

Judgment Input: A decision input of the class judgment: the output of a probabilistic model or heuristic detector about meaning or risk, such as an intent-alignment, semantic-drift, anomaly, or risk score, whoever operates the model or detector.

Verifiable-Only Outcome: The outcome that the bound policy version (DCMP2-016) yields for a request, with the authorization, obligations, consequence-budget debits, and human-approval requirements that go with it, when the policy is evaluated over the request's operation, target, and parameters and its available verifiable inputs alone, with every actor_supplied and judgment input treated as absent. A required actor_supplied or judgment input does not change the verifiable-only outcome; whether it was available affects only the decision's outcome (ECL1N-0182).

Strictness Order: The order of decision outcomes from least to most strict: admit, challenge, hold, deny. A challenge is less strict than a hold because the requester's principal can end it by supplying the evidence it names (DCMP2-019 to DCMP2-021), while a hold permits no release until a separately defined condition changes that the requester cannot bring about by answering (DCMP2-018). One outcome is at least as strict as another when it comes later in this order, or when both are deny. One hold is at least as strict as another hold, and one challenge as another challenge, when its condition for progress is met only when the other's is, its deadline is no later, and its timeout disposition is at least as strict, one that releases nothing that the other's does not release (DCMP2-018); two hold or two challenge outcomes are equally strict only when their conditions for progress, deadlines, and timeout dispositions are the same. One admit is at least as strict as another only when its authorization is contained in the other's on every dimension of ECL1N-0031, in its validity interval, and in its use policy and maximum uses; it carries every obligation of the other; it debits each consequence budget by at least as much and no later; and every human-approval threshold that applies to the other applies to it with, for the same parameter or measure and the same unit, a value no higher and at least as many approvers, and a threshold that applies only to it makes it no less strict (ECL1N-0177). A threshold in another unit is not comparable, and the admit is then not at least as strict. An outcome that is not at least as strict as another is less strict than it.

Typed Authority: Transaction-bound authority scoped by principal context, resource target, operation set, and time window.

Delegation Chain: An auditable sequence of authority transfer events between principals, including attenuation constraints.

Delegation Attenuation: Narrowing or preserving authority across a delegation hop by external Authority-layer logic. Actor assertions about their own attenuation are not sufficient proof.

Confused Deputy Condition: A failure mode where an intermediary executes actions using authority broader than caller intent.

Capability Manifest: The declared actionable surface of a run of the governed AI system, including model version or class, tool graph, reachable plugins or APIs, memory class, prompt class, credential class, and execution substrate relevant to consequential action.

Capability Freeze: The control condition in which the actionable capability surface of an authorized run is fixed for the duration of that run; material changes invalidate prior authority and require re-attestation or re-approval.

4.4 Enforcement and Provenance Terms

Soft Enforcement: Enforcement in operating-system, middleware, gateway, or service layers that the governed AI system cannot modify, disable, or bypass, but that shares the host platform's trust root.

Hard Enforcement: Enforcement implemented through an independent trust boundary such as a TEE, hardened hypervisor path, hardware security boundary, out-of-band gateway, or equivalent mechanism whose compromise is materially harder than compromise of the governed AI system.

Release Boundary: The point at which an enforcement point lets an admitted consequential action proceed toward its effect, after which refusing the action no longer stops it: for example the forwarding of a call to a remote server, the admission instant of a transaction, or a resource server's acceptance of a presented token (ECL1N-0185).

Effect Boundary: The point at which a released action's external effect takes place, such as the commit of a transaction, a remote server's execution of a forwarded call, or a physical actuation. It can follow the release boundary, as a remote server's execution follows a gateway's forwarding of the call, or be bound to it, as a transaction binds its commit to its admission instant.

Assessor-Approved Equivalent Control: An alternative implementation that does not match the reference technical mechanism but that preserves the same security and governance property under documented assumptions, residual differences, and assessor-controlled equivalence rationale.

Agent Principal: A non-human principal treated as untrusted by default and subject to the same authorization and audit rigor as human principals.

Workload Identity: Cryptographically attested identity for service or agent workloads.

Evidence Bundle: A signed record of one mediated action including principal context, policy context, decision, execution outcome, integrity metadata, and timing metadata.

Consequential Action Trace: The ordered, reconstructable record of one approved action chain from decision and authorization through all resulting external effects, including root trace identifier, per-action identifiers, and parent-child linkage.

Provenance Graph: A structured relation model for agent, activity, entity, delegation, and derivation links relevant to action reconstruction.

Transparency Log: An append-oriented verification surface used to detect post-hoc tampering.

Evidence Granularity Tier: The evidence density that applies to a class of actions or events (ECL1N-0048, ECL1N-0145).

Consequence Budget: An externally enforced quantitative limit on the amount of consequential change a run of the governed AI system may cause; reaching it brings slowdown, restricted mode, quarantine, or halt.

Consequence Budget Ledger: The retained record of budget classes, units, debits, remaining allowance, exhaustion behavior, and reset authority for a bounded consequential action class.

Irreversibility Class: The declared reversibility category for a consequential action class, such as reversible, partially reversible, or irreversible, used to determine required commit discipline and fallback rigor.

Commit Mode: The declared release pattern for a consequential action class, such as fast path, escrow with dwell time, or prepare/lock/commit, chosen in light of irreversibility and sector risk.

4.5 Deployment and Conformance Terms

Conformance Profile: A level or profile defining required controls and evidence expectations.

Full-System Mediation Claim: A claim that all reachable consequential action paths for the SoI are in scope and mediated.

Bounded Action-Class Mediation Claim: A claim that only named consequential action classes are in scope and mediated.

Component or Profile Implementation Claim: A claim about one control component or one interoperability profile without asserting end-to-end runtime mediation of a whole SoI.

Negative Boundary Annex: The disclosure artifact listing excluded effect channels, excluded tools/resources, shared surfaces, and other out-of-scope paths that a reader might otherwise over-read as covered.

Control Independence Class A: Same-operator separated controls with distinct roles, keys, or privilege domains and auditable change boundaries, subject to FR-001's prohibition on a single power controlling both planes. The same organization can administer both planes; a single administrative credential, role or host root that can alter both planes does not satisfy it.

Control Independence Class B: Control approval and evidence verification both occur in a separately administered trust domain that the governed AI system and its ordinary operators cannot unilaterally modify.

Control Independence Class C: Every Class B condition holds, and control approval and evidence verification are both held in an externally independent trust domain or authority outside the normal operator boundary of the SoI.

Federated Deployment: Multi-domain deployment with explicit trust and policy coordination interfaces.

Sovereign Deployment: Deployment where the operator keeps direct control of policy, keys, and evidence location.

Offline-Capable Deployment: Deployment that preserves enforcement behavior under network disconnection through bounded local control state.

Dependency Model: A claim's declaration of the dependencies whose loss can remove the required proof of each in-scope action class, directly or through other dependencies, and of the enclosing scope to restrict when the scope of such a loss cannot be established (ECL1N-0192).

Trusted Time Or Secure Sequencing: A trusted time source or an equivalent monotonic sequencing mechanism sufficient to order decisions, revocations, evidence creation, and verifier observations without permitting silent timestamp forgery at the claimed assurance level.

Heterogeneous Checker Quorum: A profile-tightened approval or verification pattern in which a high-consequence action proceeds only when materially different checkers, witnesses, or policy engines agree, reducing monoculture failure risk.

4.6 Interoperability Baselines

Decision and Control Message Protocol 2 (DCMP-2): The interoperable decision and control protocol for ECL-1. It defines request, response, challenge, cancellation, revocation, supersession, workflow, authority, timing, extension, integrity-policy, and safe-state semantics through the normative companion set referenced in Section 3.

Evidence Bundle and Manifest Protocol 2 (EBMP-2): The interoperable event, receipt, lifecycle, and bundle-manifest protocol for ECL-1. It defines causal closure, attempts, terminal and uncertain outcomes, human intervention, verification, fleet state, redaction, retention, export, supersession, revocation, legal hold, and destruction semantics through the normative companion set referenced in Section 3.

AIBOM Minimum Profile (AIBOM-MIN-1): The minimum content of an AI bill of materials used as part of an ECL-1 conformance claim (ECL1N-0030).

Delegation Attenuation Rule: The rule that each delegation hop preserves or narrows the parent authority (ECL1N-0031).

5. Architectural Components

ECL1N-0032§ — ECL-1 defines a logical control architecture. Deployment packaging MAY vary, but each required control responsibility MUST be assigned to an identified implementation, principal, privilege domain, and trust root, with test evidence. A required control responsibility MUST NOT be implemented by the governed AI system, and the governed AI system MUST NOT be able to rewrite, disable, or bypass one.

The ECL reference architecture comprises six responsibilities with defined interfaces. Decision, Authority, Enforcement, and Provenance are the runtime action-governance responsibilities. Verification and Fleet are supervisory control planes.

5.1 Runtime Control Invariant (Normative)

ECL1N-0028§ — All in-scope consequential actions MUST cross the Enforcement Boundary before execution.

ECL1N-0033§ — Every consequential action listed as in scope in the Consequential Action Register MUST traverse the Enforcement Layer before external effect.

For each governed action:

  1. ECL1N-0034§ — The Decision Layer MUST supply the applicable policy outcome or policy evaluation context.
  2. ECL1N-0035§ — The Authority Layer MUST supply or validate bounded right-to-act.
  3. ECL1N-0036§ — The Enforcement Layer MUST apply the resulting control outcome before execution.
  4. ECL1N-0037§ — The Provenance Layer MUST record action evidence and control context.

ECL1N-0141§ — A conformant implementation MUST NOT provide an undisclosed bypass path around the Enforcement Layer for in-scope actions, and MUST NOT suppress the required Decision, Authority, or Provenance functions associated with those actions.

ECL1N-0038§ — If an effect path exists but is excluded from the claim, it MUST appear in the Negative Boundary Annex. Omitted paths are treated as claim defects, not as implicit exclusions.

ECL1N-0039§ — Evidence of complete mediation is always bounded by the declared path- enumeration method, exercised negative tests, and the reachable capability inventory available at assessment time. A conformant claim MUST therefore document the enumeration method, exercised interception points, and any residual untested path classes rather than presenting no-bypass as a mere assertion or as a metaphysically complete proof.

5.2 Decision Layer

The Decision Layer computes admit, deny, hold, or challenge outcomes from policy and context.

ECL1N-0040§ — The Decision Layer MUST:

  • evaluate machine-readable, versioned policy;
  • produce reproducible outcomes from the logged request state, policy version, trust state, and contextual inputs used at decision time;
  • return outcome metadata sufficient for downstream enforcement and audit.

ECL1N-0041§ — Model self-report is an actor_supplied input: it MAY be one context input, but MUST NOT be the basis for authorizing any action (ECL1N-0179).

ECL1N-0164§ — For each in-scope action class, the Consequential Action Register MUST state each decision input's source and class, distinguishing actor-controlled assertions from authoritative observations under Section 4.3 and ECL1N-0178. The supporting claim evidence identifies source authority and independent transition validation as ECL1N-0178 requires; these are not additional fields in the register's wire format. An admitted action's causal influence on an independently validated transition does not by itself make the resulting observation actor-supplied.

ECL1N-0165§ — For L2 and above, a decision to admit an action of a class with financial, legal, identity, infrastructure, or physical consequence MUST rest on at least one verifiable input that states a fact about the action's target or subject and comes from a system of record, an independent state witness, or an authenticated external party; the policy and trust state, the consequence budget ledger and an approval do not satisfy this requirement. The evidence of the decision MUST identify that input by reference and digest.

The one-way rule (ECL1N-0178 to ECL1N-0182) classifies every decision input and lets only verifiable inputs admit an action. Inputs that the governed AI system controls as assertions, and judgments about meaning or risk, can make an outcome stricter and nothing else. Whether a decision complies is decided by comparing its outcome with its verifiable-only outcome in the strictness order (Section 4.3): the decision complies when its outcome is at least as strict.

ECL1N-0178§ — For each in-scope action class, the Consequential Action Register MUST declare, for each decision input that ECL1N-0164 requires it to state, its class as verifiable, actor_supplied, or judgment (Section 4.3), whether the action class requires it, its source, the maximum age of its value, and, when the Decision Layer must obtain it within a set time, its deadline: the time after the Decision Layer receives a request by which it must have the input. An input whose asserted value the governed AI system produces or can overwrite without independent validation, or whose authenticity or transition checks it can modify or bypass, MUST be declared actor_supplied; the output of a probabilistic model or heuristic detector about meaning or risk MUST NOT be declared verifiable. For each verifiable input the claim MUST identify the authority, its validation rules and the controls on freshness, selection and completeness on which an admit relies. An admitted action that causes an independently validated transition MUST NOT by that fact alone make the resulting authoritative observation actor_supplied. An actor's selection or omission of authentic observations MUST NOT establish completeness that the authority does not attest or the Decision Layer independently establish. An input that reaches the Decision Layer through the request, or through any channel that the governed AI system can write, MUST be declared actor_supplied unless the Decision Layer authenticates its origin as Section 4.3 requires. The Decision Layer MUST NOT consult a decision input that the register does not declare for the action class. The register MUST declare each action class once and each decision input of a class once. For an input that is computed from other declared inputs of the class, the register MUST name those inputs, MUST NOT declare the input verifiable unless each of them is verifiable, and MUST NOT declare it computed from itself, directly or through other inputs.

The register records these declarations in the decision_inputs member of each action entry (schemas/ecl-action-register-v1.schema.json), with the maximum age in maximum_age_seconds, any deadline in deadline_milliseconds, and the inputs from which an input is computed in derived_from. An input that arrives with the request, such as a statement of the governed AI system, needs no deadline. The registered rule ecl.rule.register-declarations-consistent.v1 checks a register document against the declarations of this requirement that its schema cannot express.

ECL1N-0179§ — An actor_supplied or judgment input MUST NOT be the basis of an admit. The outcome of a decision MUST be no less strict, in the strictness order of Section 4.3, than its verifiable-only outcome. Such an input MAY make the outcome stricter, including by bringing into effect a detection response declared under ECL1N-0167 and ECL1N-0168, and MUST NOT make it less strict, select an authorization wider than the verifiable-only outcome's, remove an obligation, reduce or defer a consequence-budget debit, or raise or remove a human-approval threshold or lower the number of distinct approvers it requires.

ECL1N-0180§ — An admit MUST be justified by policy evaluated over verifiable inputs alone: its verifiable-only outcome is admit, and its authorization is that outcome's authorization or stricter.

For L2 and above, ECL1N-0165 adds that, for the consequence classes it names, the verifiable inputs on which an admit rests include one that states a fact about the action's target or subject and comes from a system of record, an independent state witness, or an authenticated external party. The policy and trust state, a consequence budget ledger, or an approval does not count toward it, although each can be a verifiable input of the same admit.

ECL1N-0181§ — The evidence of each decision MUST identify, by reference and digest, the version of the Consequential Action Register under which the decision was made, and each decision input that the Decision Layer consulted and each that the register declares for the action class, each input once, with its declared class, whether the action class requires it, whether it was available, late, unavailable, or malformed, its reference and digest when it was available, and its effect on the outcome: supports when it is part of the basis on which the verifiable-only outcome permits the action, whatever the final outcome; restricts when it made the outcome stricter; and none when it had no effect. The decision response MUST carry this record in the registered extension ecl.ext.decision-basis.v1 (DCMP2-053).

The record can also give the verifiable-only outcome, the time at which the Decision Layer received the request, and, for each input, the time at which the Decision Layer obtained it and the time its value describes. These are the Decision Layer's statements, signed with its response. A verifier checks that they agree with each other, with the outcome and, given the register, with each input's declared deadline and maximum age (DCMP2-053); it cannot check them against the sources.

ECL1N-0182§ — A declared decision input is late when the Decision Layer obtains it after its declared deadline, or when its value is older than its declared maximum age at the time of the decision; it is unavailable when the Decision Layer does not obtain it before it decides, and malformed when it does not have its declared form or its integrity cannot be verified. An input that is unavailable, late, or malformed MUST NOT be part of the basis of an admit. If the register declares the input required for the action class, the outcome MUST NOT be admit; if the register declares it optional, the input MAY make the outcome stricter and MUST NOT otherwise affect it.

A probabilistic component that contributes to decision evaluation supplies judgment inputs. Under ECL1N-0179 and ECL1N-0180 its output can make an outcome stricter but is never the basis of an admit, so the basis of every admit is policy over verifiable inputs, which a reviewer can evaluate again exactly. Where probabilistic components contribute to decision evaluation, reproducible (ECL1N-0040) means that the logged inputs, including the recorded reference and digest of each judgment input, the policy version, trust state, and decision outcome are sufficient for a competent reviewer to reconstruct and evaluate the decision basis even when the exact internal computation is not bit-for-bit deterministic.

5.3 Authority Layer

The Authority Layer binds identity context to bounded authority.

ECL1N-0042§ — The Authority Layer MUST:

  • distinguish actor and subject context in delegated actions;
  • issue or validate transaction-bound typed authority;
  • enforce delegation attenuation and validity windows;
  • support short-lived credentials with revocation capability;
  • treat actor-supplied claims about narrowed authority as untrusted input until externally validated.

ECL1N-0031§ — Across heterogeneous token or credential systems, each delegation hop MUST preserve or narrow the parent authority on operation scope, resource scope, data scope, time window, and delegation depth. If a translated token cannot prove safe semantic intersection with parent authority, the request MUST be denied rather than widened by interpretation.

Identity carrier standards and token-exchange patterns are supporting mechanisms; they are not substitutes for bounded authority semantics.

5.4 Enforcement Layer

The Enforcement Layer is the choke point where digital intent becomes external effect.

ECL1N-0043§ — The Enforcement Layer MUST:

  • intercept all in-scope consequential actions before execution;
  • apply Decision and Authority outcomes without silent fallback;
  • fail closed on control-path ambiguity, policy ambiguity, or integrity failure;
  • preserve a separately auditable control path for emergency halt or quarantine; and
  • restrict the emergency control plane to halt, quarantine, or other pre- declared containment actions rather than ordinary runtime policy editing.

ECL-1 recognizes two enforcement classes:

  • Soft Enforcement for lower-consequence action classes, where the governed AI system cannot modify, disable, or bypass the control path, but the control path shares the host platform's trust root.
  • Hard Enforcement for action classes that require an independent trust boundary from the governed AI system.

ECL1N-0044§ — Action classes with L4 consequence, irreversible physical effect, or a sector-mandated independent actuation boundary MUST use Hard Enforcement. Other action classes MAY use Soft Enforcement only when the Consequential Action Register records the rationale and residual risk.

ECL1N-0045§ — For L2 and above, if an action class with financial, legal, identity, infrastructure, network, or non-public-data disclosure consequence uses Soft Enforcement, the Consequential Action Register and Negative Boundary Annex MUST document the rationale, shared trust-boundary assumptions, and residual bypass risk for that action class. The public claim statement MUST disclose that limitation whenever it is material to the claim.

ECL1N-0046§ — A system where the governed AI system can rewrite, disable, or bypass enforcement logic MUST NOT be treated as conformant.

ECL1N-0166§ — A change that disables, bypasses, or sets to automatic approval an in-scope control, including a policy rule, an enforcement point, a human-approval step, a verification check, a consequence budget, or a detection response, MUST be approved by a person other than its requester under the claimed independence class, MUST name an expiry, and MUST be recorded as signed evidence that the Verification Layer receives.

5.4.1 Release Contract

An enforcement point releases an admitted action at one point, its release boundary, after which refusing the action no longer stops it. The effect can take place at that point or later and elsewhere. An enforcement profile is a companion profile that states how an enforcement point releases actions, such as TRANSACTIONAL-EFFECT-1 or MCP-GATEWAY-1. The requirements below state what holds at the release boundary. Each item of ECL1N-0186 restates requirements that keep their own levels and conditions; ECL1N-0186 adds that all of them hold together, at that boundary, before the release.

ECL1N-0185§ — Each enforcement profile MUST identify the release boundary at which an enforcement point under the profile releases an action, and each claim MUST identify the release boundary of each in-scope action class; each MUST distinguish the release boundary from any later effect boundary.

ECL1N-0186§ — Before an action crosses its release boundary, the enforcement point MUST have established, for that action and at that boundary:

  1. that what it releases is exactly the action that the admitted request binds, and that it is the enforcement point that the authorization names as its audience (ECL1N-0036, DCMP2-044 to DCMP2-051);
  2. that the authorization is valid at the release boundary, shown as ECL1N-0188 requires (ECL1N-0042, DCMP2-049);
  3. that the authorization's use policy permits this use, counted atomically with every other use of the same authorization by any instance or attempt, so that concurrent, repeated or recovered attempts cannot together exceed it (DCMP2-050, EBMP2-008);
  4. that the control state that the claim's mode definitions require for the release, such as the policy and trust epoch, is current within its declared staleness limit (ECL1N-0101, ECL1N-0102, FR-110);
  5. that no halt, quarantine or revocation applies to the action among those that have reached the enforcement point, or that the claim's declared timing case requires to have reached it, by that boundary (ECL1N-0053, ECL1N-0097, ECL1N-0104);
  6. that every obligation of the response whose satisfaction is before_release, a consequence-coupling witness result of required_before_release included, is satisfied by proof that is authentic, bound to the action, of a kind that the enforcement point supports, and fresh at the release boundary itself, not only when the proof was checked (DCMP2-017);
  7. that the evidence capacity that the claim requires for the action is available, the durable recording that ECL1N-0189 requires before release included (ECL1N-0047, FR-003).

A profile can establish an item in its own terms. TRANSACTIONAL-EFFECT-1 evaluates authority, time, control state and preconditions at one admission instant while mutual exclusion holds (TE1-002), and counts each charged use once (TE1-003, TE1-010). OAUTH-AUTHORITY-1 has the resource server check a presented token and count its uses in one store that every instance shares (OA1-005, OA1-011). TIMING-SAFE-STATE-1 gives the control-state and evidence items their detail: separately declared staleness budgets (TS1-012, TS1-013), the precedence of a halt over ordinary admission (TS1-025), and buffered evidence that never silently drops verifier-critical events (TS1-021, TS1-022). docs/implementer-guide.md restates ECL1N-0186 as a predicate and says what it does not show.

ECL1N-0187§ — Required proof for a release that is missing, of a kind that the enforcement point does not support, stale, failed, or bound to another action, request, audience or authorization MUST prevent the release, and the action MUST end in the declared safe state (DCMP2-017, DCMP2-041, FR-003). A signed admit, the recognition of an obligation's type, or a proof of valid form that has not been checked against the obligation MUST NOT be treated as satisfaction of an obligation that the response defers to enforcement.

ECL1N-0188§ — A timestamp used to show that an action was released while its authority was valid MUST describe the release boundary under the observation mechanism and clock policy that the enforcement profile or the claim declares for it, and MUST NOT be the time of an earlier decision, preparation, event construction or recording presented as the observation of the release. Work that can block between the enforcement point's last check of the authority and the release, such as writing or signing evidence, waiting for a lock, or obtaining and checking an obligation's proof, MUST lie within the timing argument that the profile or the claim declares for the release boundary, which MUST be one of: a check made at the release boundary with nothing that can block between it and the release; a declared bound on the delay between the check and the release that the check allows for, enforced so that no release happens once the bound has passed; or a check of the authority at the point of effect. An action whose authority cannot be established as usable at the release boundary MUST NOT be released.

A check at the release boundary shows that the authority was usable when the enforcement point released the action. Where the effect boundary is later and remote, as when a gateway forwards a call to a server, the check does not show that the server acted before the authority expired: that needs a check at the point of effect, such as a resource server's check of a presented token under OAUTH-AUTHORITY-1, or a declared bound on the delay between release and effect. Under the second timing argument the recorded release time is the final observation that precedes the release, and the release begins within the declared bound after it; the evidence carries that bound with the observation, so a verifier tests the observation plus the bound against the window (EBMP2-050, EBMP2-071). A record made before the release is evidence that a release was intended, not that it happened, and a record made after it can describe the release accurately but cannot undo a release that ought not to have happened (EBMP2-004).

5.4.2 Recovery After Failure

A record written only after an effect can be lost with the process that was to write it, and a local record cannot be made atomic with an arbitrary remote call. The requirements below make the information needed to recover an attempt durable before its release, and keep an outcome that cannot be established unresolved rather than guessed.

ECL1N-0189§ — Before it releases an action, an enforcement point MUST durably preserve the recovery information that its declared failure model requires, which includes at least the attempt's identity, the authority and the use it consumes, and the binding of the attempt to its request, so that the information survives every failure that the claim declares covered under ECL1N-0190. After a covered failure, recovery MUST identify every outstanding attempt and classify each as known to have executed, known not to have executed, or unresolved, and MUST NOT infer execution, non-execution or reusable authority solely from the absence of a terminal record or from recovery information that it cannot read in full. An unresolved attempt MUST remain recorded as unresolved, with its known side effects and its residual uncertainty, until evidence such as an authoritative status query at the point of effect resolves it (FR-107, EBMP2-013), and MUST NOT be retried automatically unless the claim shows that the point of effect applies the effect at most once, for example by honoring an idempotency key (DCMP2-011, DCMP2-026).

ECL1N-0190§ — Each claim MUST declare, for each enforcement point, which of these failures its recovery information and its evidence survive: the exit of the enforcement point's process, a crash of its host, the failure of the storage that holds them, and the loss of the evidence service that receives them. Recovery from a failure that the claim does not declare covered MUST NOT be represented as part of the claim.

The mechanism can be a durable journal written before the release, a transactional outbox, or a transaction on the same resource that commits the effect and its evidence together, as TRANSACTIONAL-EFFECT-1 does (TE1-004). A journal does not make a remote call atomic with a local write: an attempt journaled as released whose response was lost stays unresolved until an authoritative status query, the remote side's idempotency or a person resolves it. A retention period (ECL1N-0050) keeps evidence that was recorded; it does not recover evidence that was never written. TIMING-SAFE-STATE-1 governs buffered evidence and its recovery (TS1-021 to TS1-024). Recovery information that one enforcement-point instance at a time can settle keeps a second instance from recording as not released an attempt that the first is releasing, and a recovery that records its decision durably before it writes anything writes the same records when it is repeated or interrupted; information that recovery cannot read, such as a journal that does not parse or holds more than one attempt, leaves its attempt unresolved, kept and reported, with no authority restored from it (MCP-GATEWAY-1, MG1-016).

5.5 Provenance Layer

The Provenance Layer is the governance memory for mediated actions.

ECL1N-0047§ — The Provenance Layer MUST create tamper-evident evidence bundles containing at minimum:

  • actor and subject context;
  • policy and authority context used for the decision;
  • enforcement outcome and execution result;
  • root trace and per-action identifiers;
  • timestamps and integrity metadata.

ECL1N-0048§ — The Provenance Layer MUST support evidence granularity tiers:

  • summary evidence for low-consequence routine actions when reconstructability is preserved;
  • standard evidence for normal consequential actions;
  • expanded evidence for overrides, incidents, critical actions, denied critical actions, integrity failures, and post-incident forensic triggers.

ECL1N-0145§ — A producer MUST NOT use summary evidence unless reconstructability is preserved, and MUST use expanded evidence for overrides, incidents, critical actions, denied critical actions, integrity failures, and post-incident forensic triggers.

ECL1N-0049§ — Hashes or digest references MAY be used for bulky supporting artifacts, but not when doing so would prevent a competent assessor from determining whether the action was properly constrained.

ECL1N-0050§ — Evidence bundles MUST be retained for at least the longer of:

  • the active assessment cycle for the claimed level;
  • any applicable regulatory, contractual, or legal retention requirement; or
  • 12 months from creation.

ECL1N-0195§ — Where a qualified legal reviewer determines that applicable law requires earlier removal, minimization or restriction, the operator MUST follow that recorded determination rather than treat this floor, a contract, a profile or a scheduled hold as authority to disobey the law.

ECL1N-0196§ — Before changing retention the operator MUST record, to the extent lawful, the legal basis and scope, responsible legal reviewer, approving authority, affected evidence, applicable holds or contested requests, required deadline, selected transformation or destruction, replicas and residual limits, and the effect on each assessment and verification claim.

ECL1N-0197§ — The operator MUST minimize source content and identifiers separately from operational metadata, commitments and destruction records, retain only lawful accountability evidence, and MUST NOT assume a digest is anonymous.

ECL1N-0198§ — An unresolved conflict MUST be escalated for an authorized legal determination within the applicable deadline; an administrative hold MUST NOT be used to extend a legally prohibited retention.

ECL1N-0199§ — The claim MUST disclose unavailable evidence; a verifier MUST NOT treat its destruction record as the missing evidence or claim the original bundle is complete. For EBMP-2 bundle verification, the verifier MUST apply that protocol's defined verdict and primary-finding rules to the evidence present; lawful destruction MUST NOT introduce an additional success verdict. The assessor MUST separately record the consequence for the claim under Section 7.6, preserving the distinction between assessment status and a bundle's verification verdict.

ECL1N-0051§ — Sector profiles MAY extend but MUST NOT shorten this minimum except under the documented legal-conflict procedure of ECL1N-0195 to ECL1N-0199.

5.6 Verification Layer

The Verification Layer provides continuous assurance that ECL controls still operate as designed.

ECL1N-0052§ — The Verification Layer MUST:

  • monitor enforcement and policy integrity;
  • detect drift between approved and active control state;
  • export reviewable evidence without requiring trust in the governed AI system;
  • trigger restricted mode, emergency halt, or equivalent pre-declared response for critical verification failures.

ECL1N-0167§ — A claim MUST declare the detections that indicate that the SoI may be acting wrongly, including verification findings, monitoring alerts, anomaly signals, and reports from people outside the SoI, and for each detection the response that applies to the affected action classes: hold, restricted mode, quarantine, or emergency halt.

ECL1N-0168§ — For L2 and above, a declared detection MUST bring its response into effect at the Decision Layer without any person's action and within the connected revocation propagation bound of the declared timing case; the response MUST be lifted only by a recorded control action.

ECL1N-0169§ — For L2 and above, the Verification Layer MUST detect, within the declared timing case, an in-scope control that is disabled, bypassed, set to automatic approval, or not operating without a change recorded under ECL1N-0166, and MUST treat it as a critical verification failure.

ECL1N-0194§ — A verifier MUST NOT imply that it performed a check that it did not perform or that lay outside its declared scope, MUST report a condition that it could not check, for lack of the information the check needs, as unchecked or unestablished and never as passed, and MUST keep cryptographic verification of a producer's assertion distinguishable from independent corroboration of the asserted event.

A signature shows who asserted a statement, not that the statement is true (EBMP2-004). An evidence bundle that verifies can still carry a release time that only its producer asserts, or an obligation that no check within the verifier's scope examined; the verifier's report says so, rather than leaving its reader to infer it from one verdict. EBMP-2 section 11 states what the offline bundle verifier checks and how it reports the evidence level of an effect (EBMP2-060, EBMP2-061).

The claimed independence class determines the trust domain in which evidence verification occurs (ECL1N-0142, ECL1N-0143).

5.7 Fleet Layer

The Fleet Layer governs multi-instance behavior, including a fleet of one.

ECL1N-0053§ — The Fleet Layer MUST:

  • distribute policy state with version control;
  • propagate urgent revocations within the declared timing case;
  • support quarantine or coordinated shutdown for compromised instances;
  • support staged rollout or an equivalent blast-radius control mechanism.

ECL1N-0054§ — The Fleet Layer SHOULD support rollback on verification failure.

ECL1N-0170§ — A change to the policy, the Consequential Action Register, the tool or capability manifest reachable by the SoI, an enforcement point's configuration, the trust policy, or a declared detection response MUST be reviewed and approved by a person other than its author before it takes effect, and the review MUST be recorded with the digest of the change.

ECL1N-0171§ — For L2 and above, each change to the governed AI system's model, system instructions, or tool set MUST be recorded with a version identifier and digest, and each decision request MUST identify the version of the governed AI system that requested the action.

5.8 Packaging and Independence Constraints

ECL1N-0055§ — Layers MAY be co-located in one runtime product, but implementations MUST demonstrate analyzable separation of:

  • decision versus enforcement responsibility;
  • authority issuance versus authority use;
  • evidence generation versus evidence verification;
  • ordinary runtime mediation versus the emergency control plane.

ECL-1 defines three control-independence classes:

  • Class A: same-operator separated controls with distinct roles, keys, or privilege domains and auditable cross-plane changes;
  • Class B: control approval and evidence verification both in a separately administered trust domain;
  • Class C: every Class B condition, with control approval and evidence verification both in an externally independent trust domain or authority.

ECL1N-0056§ — The minimum independence class for a claim is defined in Section 7. Class A is same-operator separation only. It is not the same thing as separate administration or external independence and MUST NOT be represented as satisfying those stronger properties. At every class, a claim MUST give a capability matrix for the governed AI, ordinary operators, administrators, credential and key custodians, and host or hypervisor roots. It MUST state which powers can alter each plane, which changes require separate credentials or authorities, and what host compromise defeats. Co-location MUST NOT be used as an exception to FR-001: if one shared host root or administrative role can alter both AI behavior and effective in-scope control outcomes, no class is satisfied, even when ordinary runtime credentials differ. Separate process names, audit logs or keys that the shared root can read or replace do not establish separation. Soft enforcement by itself does not establish FR-001.

5.9 Interoperability and Companion Artifacts

ECL1N-0057§ — The Decision Layer MUST implement at least DCMP-2 and the Provenance Layer MUST implement at least EBMP-2.

The companion interoperability artifact set defines:

  • machine-readable schemas;
  • strict parsing and restricted canonical JSON rules;
  • the ECL Integrity Envelope and algorithm registry;
  • extension, error, state, and semantic-rule registries;
  • distributed workflow, timing, safe-state, and evidence-lifecycle semantics;
  • version and compatibility rules;
  • failure behavior for invalid, expired, or semantically incomplete artifacts;
  • positive and negative conformance vectors with expected results.

ECL1N-0058§ — For L2 and above, the companion interoperability artifact set, which is part of the normative assessment surface at every level (ECL1N-0022), MUST include a public vector-coverage manifest. Every schema branch and semantic rule MUST have a positive case where applicable and at least one negative or mutation case that fails for the intended primary error. Passing an arbitrary vector count without rule coverage is insufficient.

ECL1N-0059§ — Canonical JSON is the mandatory Version 1 interchange floor. A CBOR/COSE, transport, or sector-specific representation MAY be used only through a versioned profile with independent vectors and an explicit, loss-audited mapping to the mandatory semantics. Wire-format variation MUST NOT weaken payload-bound integrity, unknown-critical-extension behavior, causal closure, or safe-state behavior.

5.10 Consequence-Coupling Controls

ECL1N-0060§ — The six-responsibility architecture remains the ECL core. Profiles MAY tighten the coupling between cognition and external reality through additional control primitives that cut across the core layers rather than replace them.

These profile-tightening consequence-coupling controls include:

  • independent precondition and postcondition state witnesses;
  • frozen capability manifests for authorized runs;
  • consequence budgets with explicit exhaustion behavior;
  • irreversibility classes and commit modes;
  • heterogeneous checker quorums for high-consequence actions.

These controls strengthen how Decision, Authority, Enforcement, Provenance, Verification, and Fleet interact with real-world state. They are cross-layer requirements, not additional architecture layers, and they do not replace the core runtime control invariant in Section 5.1.

Two consequence-coupling controls apply whether or not a profile invokes them: ECL1N-0172 makes the aggregate effect of each action class explicit, and ECL1N-0173 bounds it at L2 and above. A decision response reports the results of the coupling controls an action invokes in the registered extension ecl.ext.consequence-coupling.v1 (DCMP2-052).

ECL1N-0172§ — For each in-scope action class, the Consequential Action Register MUST state the largest aggregate effect that the SoI could cause through that class in one hour and in one day if every individual action were admitted, in units that measure the consequence, such as count, monetary value, records, recipients, or resource capacity.

ECL1N-0173§ — For L2 and above, each in-scope action class with financial, legal, data-disclosure, communication, identity, infrastructure, or physical consequence MUST be subject to at least one consequence budget that limits the aggregate count, rate, or value of its admitted actions over a declared period. The budget MUST be enforced outside the governed AI system, MUST count the actions of every session, workflow, and instance of the SoI, and MUST cause a deny or hold decision for any action that would exceed it.

5.11 Human Approval (Normative)

ECL1N-0174§ — When a person approves or rejects a consequential action, the approval MUST be made on a human-readable presentation of its operation, target, every parameter material to its consequences, the policy outcome and reasons, and the verifiable inputs on which the decision rested. A digest MUST NOT substitute for those fields. The presentation MUST show applicable amounts, units, currency, rounding, recipient or account identity, resource or deletion scope, audience and privilege changes without ambiguous labels or silent truncation; localization and accessible alternatives MUST preserve that meaning. The claim MUST declare the consequential fields for each action class and give the approver access to the complete parameters. The presentation MUST be bound to the canonical action released, and a change to its operation, target or consequential parameters MUST invalidate the approval and require a new presentation and approval before release. The evidence MUST record the approver, the presentation's reference and digest, its time, the bound action identity and digest, and the decision; the retained presentation MUST be available to the assessor subject to the retention procedure of ECL1N-0195 to ECL1N-0199.

ECL1N-0175§ — For L2 and above, a claim MUST declare a minimum review time for each action class that requires approval, and an approval recorded sooner than that time after its presentation MUST NOT release the action.

ECL1N-0176§ — For L3 and above, the claimant MUST measure, for each approver and action class, the approval rate and the review times, and MUST treat a declared anomaly, such as more consecutive approvals without a rejection than a declared limit or review times below the declared minimum, as a verification finding that triggers review of that approver's decisions.

ECL1N-0177§ — When the Consequential Action Register declares a human-approval threshold for an action class (ECL1N-0007), the Decision Layer MUST NOT return admit for an action whose measure is at or above the threshold's value until the approvals of as many distinct authorized persons as the threshold requires are recorded; until then it MUST return hold or challenge, or deny where its verifiable-only outcome or another requirement of this standard requires a deny.

The register declares the thresholds of an action class in the human_approval_threshold member of its entry, a list of one or more thresholds (schemas/ecl-action-register-v1.schema.json). Each threshold names what it compares in one of two ways: parameter, an RFC 6901 JSON Pointer to a value in the request's action.parameters, or measure, a URI naming a measure that the deployment defines, such as a consequence dimension or a value computed from several parameters, whose definition is reviewed with the register (EA-20). It also gives the unit, the value at or above which an action needs approval, as a decimal string, and the number of distinct authorized persons whose approval it needs. An action is at or above a parameter threshold when the value at the pointer is a non-negative integer, or a string in the decimal form of value, that is at or above value, the two compared as exact decimals; when the pointer names no value, or a value of another form, a negative number among them, the action counts as at or above it. An action at or above several thresholds needs, for each of them, the approvals that it requires; one person's approval counts toward each threshold that it applies to.

7.9 Assessment Status Hierarchy

The conformance assessment taxonomy is hierarchical:

  1. Section 11 checklist items are scored Pass, Fail, or Not Applicable.
  2. Failed checklist items receive finding severity such as critical, major, or minor.
  3. Aggregated findings determine the claim status in Section 7.6.
  4. Claim status informs procurement gate decisions in Section 11.4.

ECL1N-0081§ — This hierarchy MUST be preserved in conformance reports so checklist results, finding severity, claim status, and procurement decisions are not conflated. Public claim statements MUST preserve the same hierarchy rather than collapsing all outcomes into one generic compliance label.

7.10 Overclaim Boundaries

Conformance level statements:

  • ECL1N-0082§ — MUST describe ECL runtime-control maturity only;
  • ECL1N-0083§ — MUST cite both claimed level and claim boundary class;
  • ECL1N-0084§ — MUST NOT be represented as automatic legal compliance or sector certification;
  • ECL1N-0085§ — MUST distinguish direct control evidence from interpretive mappings.

7.11 Finalized Assessment Baselines

ECL1N-0147§ — An assessor MUST classify each failed checklist item with one of these severities:

  • critical: bypassable enforcement, undisclosed effect path, fail-open behavior, absent required evidence chain, broken emergency halt path, or equivalent condition that invalidates the claimed level.
  • major: a material failure of a required control that does not rise to critical but blocks full conformance at the claimed level.
  • minor: a bounded caveat, documentation shortfall, or localized weakness that does not defeat the required control when explicitly disclosed and accepted by the assessor.

Assessment competency and independence baseline:

  • ECL1N-0148§ — The assessment MUST be signed by a reviewer who is not the primary implementer for the assessed SoI; at L1 it MAY be internal.
  • ECL1N-0086§ — L2: independent assessor or assessor team with no direct implementation, managed-operation, or claim-approval authority over the SoI during the assessment period or prior 24 months; compensation MUST NOT be contingent on claim issuance; material affiliate or reseller relationships with the assessed SoI operator MUST be disclosed and are disqualifying unless a sector authority explicitly permits and publishes the exception.
  • ECL1N-0149§ — L3: the assessor MUST meet the L2 baseline and MUST demonstrate the ability to conduct adversarial architecture pressure testing.
  • ECL1N-0150§ — L4: the assessor MUST meet the L3 baseline and MUST demonstrate sector-domain competence and formal-assurance review competence appropriate to the deployment profile.

ECL1N-0151§ — For L2 and above, the assessment MUST include at least these negative tests:

  • attempted bypass of the enforcement path;
  • attempted fail-open on missing proof or invalid contract;
  • emergency halt or quarantine replay;
  • validation of the Negative Boundary Annex against reachable effect paths;
  • revocation and restricted-mode transition test.

7.12 Versioning, Compatibility, and Reassessment

ECL1N-0087§ — Every claim MUST declare:

  • the ECL-1 core version;
  • the companion interoperability artifact version set;
  • any sector profile version invoked.

ECL1N-0088§ — Breaking changes to the core standard or companion interop profiles MUST provide at least a 12-month deprecation window unless an emergency security fix requires faster withdrawal.

ECL1N-0152§ — A claim MUST be reassessed after any of the following:

  • any critical finding in a claimed deployment;
  • material change to enforcement placement, trust roots, authority semantics, or evidence-verification path;
  • repeated breach of the declared timing case within a rolling 90-day period;
  • replacement of the major model, major tool-execution substrate, or policy engine in the claimed SoI.

8. Deployment Models

ECL-1 permits multiple deployment topologies. The deployment model changes placement and trust boundaries, but it does not change the control law defined in Section 5.

8.1 Cross-Model Invariants (Normative)

ECL1N-0089§ — Regardless of topology, a conformant deployment MUST:

  • preserve Decision, Authority, Enforcement, Provenance, Verification, and Fleet responsibilities;
  • enforce no-bypass mediation for in-scope consequential actions;
  • fail closed on missing proof or control ambiguity;
  • produce reviewable action evidence suitable for independent assessment;
  • declare whether each in-scope action class uses Soft Enforcement or Hard Enforcement.

ECL1N-0090§ — A deployment that only observes behavior after execution, without a pre-action enforcement boundary, MUST NOT claim mediation conformance.

8.2 Model A: Centralized Control Plane with Distributed Enforcement

Decision, Authority, and Fleet functions are centrally operated; Enforcement runs near the workloads of the governed AI system.

ECL1N-0091§ — This model SHOULD be used where:

  • low-latency policy updates are required across many instances;
  • central audit operations and rapid revocation are operational priorities.

Key caveat: centralized control-plane outages can become fleet-wide risk unless bounded degraded-mode behavior is defined.

8.3 Model B: Federated Multi-Domain Governance

Multiple administrative domains coordinate trust and policy while retaining local authority boundaries.

ECL1N-0092§ — This model MUST define:

  • domain trust-bundle exchange and update procedures;
  • delegation semantics across domain boundaries;
  • cross-domain evidence verification expectations;
  • timing behavior that satisfies the declared timing case.

Key caveat: federation establishes recognition, not automatic authorization.

8.4 Model C: Sovereign Self-Managed Deployment

All six responsibilities are operated under one organization's direct control.

ECL1N-0093§ — This model SHOULD be used where data residency, regulatory, or mission constraints require local control of policy, keys, and evidence stores.

Key caveat: sovereign operation increases local operational burden for verification, incident response, and key lifecycle discipline.

8.5 Model D: Offline-Capable Edge or Embedded Deployment

Enforcement remains local during disconnection; Decision, Authority, and Fleet inputs may rely on cached bounded state.

ECL1N-0094§ — This model MUST define:

  • maximum policy and trust-cache staleness thresholds;
  • transition behavior when stale-state thresholds are exceeded;
  • post-reconnect reconciliation of policy and provenance state.

Key caveat: stale local policy can preserve safety while degrading availability.

ECL1N-0095§ — The claim MUST state which actions stop and which continue when cached state goes stale.

8.6 Model E: Air-Gapped or High-Assurance Segmented Deployment

Control updates and evidence export occur through controlled signed transfer channels rather than persistent network links.

ECL1N-0096§ — This model MUST include:

  • integrity verification for update media and evidence export packages;
  • dual-control or equivalent safeguards for emergency policy changes;
  • documented revocation and incident workflows compatible with disconnection.

Key caveat: strong isolation can slow revocation propagation and cross-site forensics unless procedures are engineered in advance.

8.7 Declared Timing Case (Normative)

ECL1N-0097§ — Each conformance claim MUST publish a declared timing case covering the following control intervals. For each bound, the case MUST state the TS1-034 elements: observation points, objective type, measuring clock, and load and fault conditions:

  • connected revocation propagation;
  • maximum offline control-state staleness before restricted mode or halt;
  • delay from first approved transfer path to evidence export completion.

A sector profile or engineering timing case MAY adopt stricter values, and the claim packet MUST then publish the rationale, control assumptions, and residual risk. A claim MUST NOT adopt a less strict value unless, in addition, its assessor has approved the deviation and the claim packet records that approval.

Claimed Level Default connected revocation propagation Default offline control-state staleness before restricted mode Default delay from first approved transfer path to evidence export completion
L1 24 hours 24 hours 72 hours
L2 15 minutes 4 hours 24 hours
L3 5 minutes 1 hour 4 hours
L4 60 seconds 15 minutes 1 hour

These defaults are revocation-propagation deadlines, distinct from halt bounds under TS1-035, and a claim declares its halt acknowledgment bound separately (TS1-036).

ECL1N-0099§ — For federated deployments, trust-bundle updates and revocation exchange across domains MUST satisfy the same connected revocation timing case.

ECL1N-0100§ — For L3 and L4 claims, revocation timing, evidence ordering, and verifier replay MUST rely on trusted time or secure monotonic sequencing sufficient to prevent the governed AI system from silently forging or reordering control timestamps.

8.8 Operational Fallback States (Normative)

ECL1N-0101§ — Every claim MUST define:

  • Degraded Mode entry conditions;
  • Restricted Mode entry conditions and allowed actions;
  • Emergency Halt or equivalent safe-state conditions.

At minimum:

  • ECL1N-0102§ — Degraded Mode MAY continue only bounded actions under signed cached state;
  • ECL1N-0103§ — Restricted Mode MUST deny all new consequential actions except explicit safe-state or containment actions;
  • ECL1N-0104§ — Emergency Halt MUST take precedence when integrity failure or operator safety requires cessation of consequential action execution.

ECL1N-0029§ — When the degraded-mode window expires or a critical integrity failure occurs, a system in Degraded Mode MUST transition to Restricted Mode or Emergency Halt.

ECL1N-0146§ — Fallback states MUST take precedence in the order Emergency Halt > Restricted Mode > Degraded Mode > ordinary operation, and each trigger condition in the following table MUST bring at least the state the table gives for it.

Trigger Condition Minimum Required State
Signed control state still valid, dependency degraded, no critical integrity failure Degraded Mode
Cached state expired, proof missing, or declared degraded-mode window exceeded Restricted Mode
Critical integrity failure, emergency control activation, or operator safety requires cessation Emergency Halt

ECL1N-0105§ — An implementation MAY transition to a stricter state earlier than this table requires. It MUST NOT remain in a weaker state once the corresponding trigger condition is met.

The trigger "proof missing" in the table of ECL1N-0146 concerns a fault after which required proof cannot be obtained for one or more action classes. A single action whose required proof is missing, such as an action whose before-release obligation is unproven, is refused under ECL1N-0187 and DCMP2-017 whatever the mode. ECL1N-0191 to ECL1N-0193 set which action classes such a fault restricts and when the restriction ends, so that two assessors reach the same scope for the same fault. Within the scope of a restriction, Restricted Mode has the effect that ECL1N-0103 gives it (Section 4.2).

ECL1N-0191§ — The state that ECL1N-0146 requires for missing proof, for a critical integrity failure and for an emergency control activation MUST cover at least the scope that the following table gives for the fault, determined under the claim's dependency model (ECL1N-0192), and a fault that matches more than one row MUST bring the widest scope and the strictest state of the rows it matches. An action class outside the scope MUST continue only while all of its own required proof (TS1-031) and controls are intact, and a dependency that supplies an action class's required proof MUST NOT be treated as advisory for that class in order to keep the class outside the scope (TS1-032).

Fault or condition Minimum scope and state
Required proof unobtainable for one action class, from a dependency that no other in-scope action class requires Restricted Mode for that action class
A dependency that supplies required proof to more than one action class fails or goes stale Restricted Mode for every action class that requires the dependency, directly or through other dependencies
Critical integrity failure or emergency control activation The state that ECL1N-0146 gives the trigger, for the whole deployment and in its order of precedence; no action-class scope or local exception applies
Required proof lost where the scope of the loss cannot be established Restricted Mode for the enclosing scope that the dependency model declares for the dependency, and for the whole deployment where it declares none

ECL1N-0192§ — Each claim MUST declare its dependency model with its mode definitions (ECL1N-0101): for each in-scope action class, every dependency whose loss can remove its required proof, directly or through another dependency, and for each such dependency the enclosing scope of action classes to restrict when the scope of a loss of the proof it supplies cannot be established. A dependency that the model names MUST be declared, wherever the claim declares its dependencies, a safe-state profile document included, as one that supplies required proof (TS1-031).

ECL1N-0193§ — The claim's mode definitions MUST state, for each trigger of ECL1N-0146, which authorization, if any, is needed to lift the restriction that the trigger brings. A restriction MUST NOT be lifted for an action class until required proof for that class has been obtained again after the fault, not taken from state cached or recorded before it, and until that authorization, or one that another requirement of this standard requires, has been recorded; a deployment-wide state brought by a critical integrity failure or an emergency control activation MUST be lifted only by a recorded control action that the claim's mode definitions authorize (ECL1N-0168, TS1-014, TS1-027).

Informative examples (non-normative):

  • A deployment admits payments and calendar changes. Payments rest on an account balance from a system of record that calendar changes do not use. When the system of record cannot be reached, payments are refused, and calendar changes continue with their own required proof intact (first row).
  • Both classes take their decisions from one decision service. When it stops answering, both classes are restricted until it answers again with fresh decisions (second row); the outage steps of the incident replay suite (record-1-knight-2012, record-17-operator-2025) replay this case.
  • The enforcement point finds that its policy bundle has been altered. The whole deployment enters Emergency Halt, whatever action-class scopes it declares (third row).
  • A cache of signed state witnesses that several classes share fails, and the deployment cannot tell whose witnesses it held. It restricts the enclosing scope that its dependency model declares for the cache, or the whole deployment where the model declares none (fourth row).

8.9 Procurement and Adoption Guidance (Informative)

Source-grounded procurement patterns support staged adoption:

  • sandbox or testbed deployment before broad scale-up;
  • explicit incident reporting and evidence-retention expectations;
  • maturity progression rather than one-step certification claims.

These are adoption practices, not substitutes for runtime mediation controls.

8.10 Profile Extension Rules

  • ECL1N-0106§ — Sector profiles MAY tighten the default timing baselines or the required enforcement class for a named action class.
  • ECL1N-0107§ — Sector profiles MAY require a State Witness Register, Capability Freeze, Consequence Budget Ledger, Irreversibility Class with declared Commit Mode, or Heterogeneous Checker Quorum for specific action classes.
  • ECL1N-0108§ — Federated cross-vendor deployments MUST exchange at minimum: trust-domain identifier, issuer or trust-bundle reference, decision-contract version, root trace identifier, actor and subject context, authorization or capability reference, resource scope, expiry, policy reference, and evidence profile identifier.
  • ECL1N-0184§ — Where a federated cross-vendor deployment exchanges an equivalent representation of an item that ECL1N-0108 lists, the representation MUST be semantically lossless or strictly narrower than the declared baseline.

9. Threat Model and Security Considerations

This section defines threat assumptions and required security behavior for ECL runtime governance.

9.1 Threat Scope

ECL-1 threat scope includes attacks on:

  • decision integrity;
  • authority and delegation integrity;
  • enforcement boundary integrity;
  • provenance integrity and audit verifiability;
  • verification integrity;
  • fleet-wide control propagation;
  • emergency control-plane misuse.

This section addresses runtime safety-enforcement posture. It does not claim to solve all liveness, availability, or legal-compliance outcomes by architecture alone.

9.2 Baseline Adversary Model

ECL1N-0109§ — Conforming implementations MUST assume at least:

  • malicious or compromised agent principals;
  • compromised plugins, tools, or upstream dependencies;
  • external attackers with network and supply-chain access attempts;
  • privileged insider misuse against policy, keys, or evidence surfaces;
  • boundary manipulation by a claimant seeking to narrow scope while preserving marketing language.

9.3 Threat Categories and Layered Controls

9.3.1 Bypass and Side-Channel Execution

Risk: consequential actions execute outside the Enforcement Layer.

Required response:

  • complete mediation checks;
  • path enumeration and negative-boundary disclosure;
  • deny-by-default on uncertain routing.

9.3.2 Confused Deputy and Delegation Abuse

Risk: intermediary actors misuse broad or ambiguous delegated authority.

Required response:

  • transaction-bound typed authority;
  • actor/subject separation in decision contracts;
  • external delegation attenuation and expiry constraints.

9.3.3 Decision or Policy Poisoning

Risk: corrupted policy inputs or decision semantics create unsafe allows.

Required response:

  • versioned policy with approval provenance;
  • reproducible decision evaluation;
  • verification alerts on policy drift.

9.3.4 Credential Theft and Replay

Risk: long-lived or replayable authority artifacts enable unauthorized actions.

Required response:

  • short-lived credentials;
  • explicit audience/resource binding;
  • revocation propagation and freshness checks.

9.3.5 Provenance Tampering or Evidence Flooding

Risk: post-hoc evidence rewriting hides unauthorized behavior, or excessive low-value evidence volume makes verification unusable.

Required response:

  • signed tamper-evident evidence bundles;
  • tiered evidence granularity;
  • independent evidence export and review paths.

9.3.6 Common-Mode Fleet Compromise

Risk: one control failure pattern scales across many instances.

Required response:

  • staged rollout controls;
  • fleet quarantine and coordinated shutdown paths;
  • verification signals aggregated across instances for anomaly detection.

9.3.7 Language-Mediated Boundary Manipulation and Prompt Injection

Risk: hostile prompts, retrieved content, tool-returned text, or machine-consumable outputs manipulate a conversational boundary so that the system discloses protected data or materially advances consequential actions through language-mediated paths.

Required response:

  • treat conversational inputs, retrieved content, tool-returned content, and machine-consumable outputs as untrusted when they can influence consequential action release or disclosure;
  • ensure no language-originated path bypasses the Enforcement Layer for any in-scope consequential effect;
  • classify output-mediated workflow triggers, approvals, denials, notices, instructions, and machine-consumed messages in the Consequential Action Register or Negative Boundary Annex; and
  • deny or restrict execution when the conversational-to-action boundary is ambiguous or unverifiable.

9.3.8 Decision-Input Manipulation

Risk: the governed AI system steers a decision through its inputs. It can shape the content that a judgment input scores, such as an intent-alignment or anomaly score, until the score is favorable. It can pass an input it controls off as verifiable: by supplying a reference in the request, by authenticating an instruction with a credential it holds, by citing an actor-controlled assertion that an admitted action stored without independent validation, or by feeding content it wrote into a deterministic check whose result is then treated as verifiable. This differs from an authoritative source independently validating a permitted state transition: causal influence on that transition does not make its authoritative observation actor-supplied (Section 4.3). It can turn a stale value into a fresh-looking one by having a check computed from it. A record of the decision basis that omits an input, lists one twice, hides a derivation, or names no register can hide what a decision rested on, and so can a register that declares an action class twice.

Required response:

  • classify each decision input by its source and by the channel through which it reaches the Decision Layer, give an input computed from others the least trusted class among them, declare each derivation in the register, and consult no input that the register does not declare (ECL1N-0178, EA-21);
  • keep every outcome at least as strict as its verifiable-only outcome, so that actor_supplied and judgment inputs make decisions only stricter and every admit rests on policy over verifiable inputs (ECL1N-0179, ECL1N-0180);
  • record the basis of every decision against the named register version, each input once, and verify that record offline, refusing evidence that breaks the decision-basis rule or lacks the record (ECL1N-0181, DCMP2-053);
  • give an unavailable, late, or malformed input, or one computed from such an input, no part in the basis of an admit (ECL1N-0182).

9.4 Normative Security Requirements

ECL1N-0110§ — ECL implementations MUST:

  • enforce fail-closed behavior for policy ambiguity, missing proof, and integrity failure;
  • preserve architectural independence between the governed AI system's behavior and control logic;
  • treat conversational inputs, retrieved content, tool-returned content, and machine-consumable outputs as untrusted whenever they can influence consequential action release, protected-data disclosure, or downstream execution;
  • prevent privileged changes to policy or enforcement without auditable control actions across the required independence class;
  • retain evidence artifacts according to the declared evidence-granularity tier and incident response needs.

ECL1N-0111§ — Human oversight MAY supplement these controls but MUST NOT be the sole mechanism for governing consequential actions.

9.5 Privacy and Sensitive Data Considerations

ECL1N-0112§ — Security controls SHOULD minimize sensitive-data exposure:

  • secrets SHOULD be passed via reference-based mechanisms where possible;
  • ECL1N-0113§ — Evidence bundles SHOULD support redaction or partitioning while preserving auditability;
  • ECL1N-0114§ — Provenance exports SHOULD preserve required accountability with least necessary disclosure;
  • ECL1N-0115§ — If redaction hides materially relevant context, a sealed-review or escrow path MUST exist for the assessor or regulator authorized to inspect it.
  • the digest of a value that can take only a few forms, such as a score, a yes-or-no approval, or a person's identifier, lets anyone who can list the possible forms confirm which one a record holds; a digest in the decision-basis record (ECL1N-0181) or in exported evidence protects such a value only when the content it digests also holds a random value that the reader cannot guess, or when the digest is keyed with a secret that the reader does not hold, as OTEL-EVIDENCE-EXPORT-1 does for exported telemetry (OE1-010).

9.6 Operational Security Posture

ECL1N-0116§ — Deployments SHOULD implement:

  • regular red-team exercises targeting layer bypass, delegation abuse, and boundary misclassification;
  • incident workflows that preserve control/evidence artifacts for forensic use;
  • staged recovery procedures for fleet-wide revocation and policy rollback;
  • heartbeat or liveness checks that trigger the declared degraded-mode, restricted-mode, or emergency-halt path when critical control dependencies fail.

Monitoring is necessary but not sufficient: monitoring without preventive enforcement does not satisfy the ECL control objective.

9.7 Security Interoperability Baseline

  • ECL1N-0117§ — Evidence-signing interoperability MUST use ECL Integrity Envelope 1 or a separately profiled, loss-audited envelope that preserves its typed-payload, canonical-byte, payload-bound policy, key, suite, threshold, trust, revocation, and offline-verification semantics.
  • ECL1N-0118§ — DCMP-2 and EBMP-2 artifacts MUST pass strict parsing, companion schema validation, applicable semantic rules, critical-extension processing, and payload-bound integrity policy. Schema validity alone is insufficient.
  • ECL1N-0119§ — Redacted provenance exports MUST preserve verifier-critical fields: root trace identifier, action identifier, actor and subject identifier or stable pseudonymous equivalent, policy reference, decision outcome, timing metadata, causal predecessors, open or terminal action state, artifact digests, registry roots, and signature-verification material.
  • ECL1N-0120§ — Redaction MUST create a new derivative and authenticated redaction manifest. A claim MUST be downgraded or rejected if redaction removes evidence required for that claim.

9.8 Evidence-Signing Key Custody (Normative)

An evidence-signing key is a private key whose signatures a verifier accepts, under the claim's trust policy (TRUST-POLICY-1), on a decision request, decision response, evidence event, observation, or time attestation. The tamper evidence that FR-108 requires is only as strong as the custody of these keys. FR-105 governs workload identity credentials, and TRUST-POLICY-1 governs how a verifier accepts and revokes keys; this section governs how the claimant holds evidence-signing keys. ECL1N-0156 to ECL1N-0162 apply to the keys operated within the claim boundary; a time authority outside the claim boundary appears in the inventory only.

ECL1N-0155§ — A claim MUST maintain an inventory of every evidence-signing key that its trust policy accepts, giving for each key: its key identifier, its registered purposes, the trust-policy principal it is bound to, the component and key store that hold the private key, the roles and workloads that can cause it to sign, and its activation time and planned retirement time.

ECL1N-0156§ — The governed AI system MUST NOT be able to read or export a private key that signs decision responses, evidence events, observations, or time attestations, or to invoke a signing operation with such a key other than through the component to which the key is bound.

ECL1N-0157§ — A private key MUST NOT sign for more than one of the decision point, the enforcement point, an observer, and a time authority.

ECL1N-0158§ — Each evidence-signing key MUST have a declared maximum active lifetime of no more than 24 months for L1 and L2 claims and no more than 12 months for L3 and L4 claims, and MUST stop signing before that lifetime ends. A successor key MUST be accepted by a published trust-policy version before its predecessor stops signing, and the claimant MUST retain every trust-policy version under which a key signed for at least the evidence retention period of ECL1N-0050.

ECL1N-0159§ — When an evidence-signing key is known or suspected to be compromised, the claimant MUST publish a trust-policy version that revokes the key with an effective time no later than the earliest suspected time of compromise, MUST deliver that version to its verifiers within the connected revocation propagation bound of its declared timing case (Section 8.7), and MUST record the event, the revocation, and the evidence that the key signed between the effective time and the revocation's publication. The claimant MUST exercise this procedure at least once per assessment cycle. A verifier applies the revocation to every object and every time attestation that the key signed whose time, the object's own or attested time or the attestation's attested time, is at or after the revocation's effective time, whatever trust-policy version the object names.

ECL1N-0160§ — For L2 and above, an evidence-signing private key MUST be held in a key store that does not permit its export in plaintext, and every signing operation MUST be recorded, attributed to the requesting component, in a record that the governed AI system cannot modify.

ECL1N-0161§ — For L3 and above, an evidence-signing private key MUST be generated inside, and never leave, a hardware security module or an equivalent hardware-isolated key store whose isolation is attested or independently validated.

ECL1N-0162§ — For L4, generating, backing up, restoring, or destroying an evidence-signing key, and changing the trust policy that accepts it, MUST require at least two authorized individuals acting together, and each such operation MUST be recorded.

10. Relationship to Existing Standards

ECL-1 complements existing governance and security frameworks by specifying runtime architecture controls at the consequential action boundary. It does not replace management systems, legal obligations, or sector certification regimes.

10.1 Positioning Rules

  • ECL1N-0121§ — ECL-1 conformance MUST be represented as a runtime-control conformance statement, not as framework certification by proxy.
  • ECL1N-0122§ — ECL-1 mappings to external standards, laws, or guidance MUST identify both supported obligations and uncovered obligations.
  • ECL1N-0123§ — Marketing or procurement materials MUST NOT state that ECL-1 alone guarantees legal compliance with any law or regulation.
  • Mapping artifacts and crosswalks are informative companion surfaces unless a procurement or sector profile makes them co-required for a specific claim.

10.2 Mapping Claim Classes

ECL1N-0124§ — Any ECL-to-framework mapping MUST classify each mapped obligation as one of:

  • Direct runtime support: ECL control semantics directly address the named runtime obligation.
  • Supporting evidence only: ECL produces evidence or control hooks that may support compliance work but do not themselves satisfy the obligation.
  • Out of scope: the obligation remains outside ECL-1 and requires other organizational, legal, safety, or sector controls.

10.3 Minimum Mapping Contents

ECL1N-0125§ — Any public or procurement-facing mapping claim MUST include:

  • clause or article references for the mapped external surface;
  • explicit statement of the ECL control or evidence surface being relied on;
  • explicit statement of residual obligations not covered by ECL-1;
  • interpretive assumptions used in the mapping;
  • the Section 7.7 minimum public claim statement whenever the mapping is tied to a concrete ECL conformance claim;
  • validation state of the mapping artifact (draft, internally reviewed, externally reviewed, or equivalent).

ECL1N-0126§ — Mappings SHOULD be versioned and updated when source framework editions change.

10.6 Scope Statement

ECL-1 standardizes, in one runtime architecture, all of the following:

  1. pre-action mediation of consequential actions;
  2. typed, bounded, and revocable runtime authority;
  3. action-level tamper-evident provenance;
  4. fail-closed operation on missing proof;
  5. supervisory verification and fleet-response behavior;
  6. assessable claim-boundary disclosure and evidence rules.

This describes ECL-1's scope; it makes no statement about what other frameworks cover.

10.7 Boundary on Borrowed Legitimacy

ECL1N-0128§ — Framework mappings, crosswalks, and claim packets MAY support adoption. They MUST NOT be used to imply that ECL-1 has been endorsed, ratified, or certified by the external framework owner unless that endorsement actually exists.

11. Conformance Checklist

This checklist is procurement-facing and assessor-usable. It converts Section 6 controls and Section 7 claim logic into auditable acceptance criteria.

11.1 Usage Rules

  • ECL1N-0129§ — A supplier claiming ECL-1 conformance MUST submit this checklist with evidence links for all applicable items.
  • ECL1N-0130§ — Each checklist item MUST be marked Pass, Fail, or Not Applicable.
  • ECL1N-0153§ — An item MUST NOT be marked Not Applicable unless its applicability predicate is false for the SoI or the claimed level is below the item's minimum level.
  • ECL1N-0131§ — Not Applicable classifications for delegation, emergency-control, boundary-challenge, or public-claim items MUST be supported by evidence that the applicability predicate is actually false for the declared SoI.
  • ECL1N-0132§ — Runtime evidence SHOULD be current within 12 months for L1-L2 claims and within 6 months for L3-L4 claims unless the item is tied to a static certification artifact.
  • ECL1N-0133§ — Checklist submission MUST include the SoI boundary, claim boundary class, and Negative Boundary Annex defined in Section 2.
  • ECL1N-0134§ — A separate FR-to-checklist coverage matrix MUST accompany the checklist.

11.2 Rating Method

Assessor rating method:

  1. Verify claim boundary, claim boundary class, and target level.
  2. Verify the Consequential Action Register and Negative Boundary Annex before scoring any technical control.
  3. Evaluate each checklist item against required evidence and pass criteria.
  4. Record finding severity (critical, major, minor) for each failed item.
  5. Apply Section 7 claim rules for full, conditional, or assessment-only status.
  6. For complete-mediation, negative-boundary, and adversarial no-bypass items, record the test method, exercised scope, and residual untested path classes.

ECL1N-0154§ — An assessor MUST record each of the following as a critical finding:

  • bypassable enforcement for in-scope consequential actions;
  • fail-open behavior on missing proof;
  • missing action-level evidence chain for required controls;
  • broken emergency halt path;
  • undisclosed reachable effect path outside the declared claim boundary;
  • absence of required negative-testing evidence for L2 and above.

11.4 Procurement Decision Gates

Recommended gate logic:

  1. Go - all applicable items pass.
  2. Conditional Go - only L2-L3, no critical failures, remediation plan <= 180 days, required negative-testing evidence exists, and any repeated conditional period shows material closure progress on the same unresolved findings.
  3. No Go - any critical failure, undisclosed effect path, unresolved major failure at required level, or missing required assessment expectation.

ECL1N-0135§ — For L4 claims, conditional award SHOULD NOT be used where regulator or safety authority requires full closure before operation.

11.5 Checklist Deliverables Package

ECL1N-0136§ — A complete supplier package SHOULD include:

  • explicit public claim statement required by Section 7.7;
  • populated checklist table;
  • requirement-to-evidence matrix;
  • FR-to-checklist coverage matrix;
  • System of Interest diagram and trust-boundary diagram;
  • Consequential Action Register;
  • Negative Boundary Annex;
  • declared timing case;
  • architecture and privilege-boundary documentation, including privilege matrix;
  • at least one sample DCMP-2 request and one sample DCMP-2 response for the claimed slice;
  • at least one sample EBMP-2 evidence bundle and one redacted assessor-facing evidence bundle;
  • if a profile invokes consequence-coupling controls: State Witness Register, capability-manifest reference or hash, Consequence Budget Ledger, irreversibility classes with commit modes, and quorum rule declaration;
  • latest independent assessment report;
  • assessor competency and conflict-of-interest statement for L2 and above;
  • structured assessor outputs for L2 and above: claim decision, findings, residual-risk statement, and independence/conflict disclosure, or an equivalent assessor-approved format;
  • one revocation drill record and one emergency-halt or quarantine drill record for the claimed slice;
  • incident response and emergency override procedures;
  • change-management and rollout governance artifacts;
  • external-framework mapping with packet identifier, bounded obligation slice, residual-gap disclosure, and mapping validation state, if used.
