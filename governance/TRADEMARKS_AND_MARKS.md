# ECL-1 names, compatibility statements, and marks

G-14, Inc. has registered and licensed no mark for ECL-1, and ECL-1 has no
conformance or certification mark. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Descriptive use

Accurate reference to "ECL-1", to its protocol and profile names, and to
version identifiers is permitted as descriptive identification, subject to
applicable law. A party may state that it implements a named version when the
statement is bounded and not misleading. The standard is written "ECL-1" or
"ECL-1 Version 1.0", never "ECL-1.0", which is the SPDX identifier of the
Educational Community License 1.0.

The name ECL-1 names the specification, which G-14, Inc. maintains, not a
product. No product, G-14's included, is described as "ECL-1".

## Conformance claims

A conformance claim names the version, the level and the claim boundary, with
the other elements that Section 7.7 of the standard requires, and it is
self-declared unless an assessment of it is listed
([CONFORMANCE_CLAIMS](CONFORMANCE_CLAIMS.md)). A statement such as
"ECL-1 compliant", which omits those elements, is not a valid claim
(Section 7.7).

## Prohibited implication

Without separate written authority, no party may imply that:

- G-14, the working group, a regulator, or an assessor endorses the party;
- implementation, or a listing in the implementers registry, equals
  conformance, certification, safety, or legal compliance;
- a modified profile is the unmodified ECL-1 standard;
- an internal or self-assessment is independent;
- a test pass or a listing grants a mark.

A result of G-14's own tests is labelled as G-14's, and is never presented as
certification or as an independent assessment.

## Compatibility statement

The preferred form of a compatibility statement is:

> Implements the identified ECL-1 version and declared profile; conformance
> status and assessment independence are stated separately.

The statement includes exact versions, scope, deviations, and evidence date.

## No certification mark

ECL-1 has no certification mark, and the maintainer certifies nothing. A
United States certification mark cannot be held by a party that produces or
markets the goods or services that the mark certifies, and the charter
discloses the maintainer's interests ([CHARTER](CHARTER.md), section 12); a
certification program would need a separate certifying body. Assessments are
made by assessors outside G-14 under
[ASSESSOR_INDEPENDENCE](ASSESSOR_INDEPENDENCE.md). The implementers registry
lists self-declared claims, and a listing is not a mark.
